### Is the time-warped lamp test a faithful proof? (question 1)
Yes. I judge it a stronger proof than waiting for a real night and watching spawns:
- Light is a property of the world, not of the time. The time of day only enters the spawn decision through `getSkyDarken`, which
  `Monster.isDarkEnoughToSpawn` uses with a random draw. The test sets the clock every 100 ticks from 12 500 to 23 500 and reads one tick
  later, after `ServerLevel.tick` has run `updateSkyBrightness`. So it samples the whole range of darkness a real night passes through, at
  111 points with a seeded random. A real 11 000-tick wait would add only further random draws of the same predicate.
- It calls the exact predicate that `NaturalSpawner.isValidSpawnPostitionForType` calls (`SpawnPlacements.checkSpawnRules(..., NATURAL, ...)`
  plus `isSpawnPositionOk`) for zombie, skeleton, creeper and spider. That covers both `checkMonsterSpawnRules` and
  `checkSurfaceMonstersSpawnRules` users.
- It has three counterfactuals: the same lit cells spawn before the lamp (6 629), the hd-13 ring spawns (so the radius is exactly 12 and not
  larger), and a far control spawns on the night plateau. The test therefore cannot pass vacuously, for example because of a time stuck at
  day, peaceful difficulty or a wrong floor height.
- Mutations A and B both go red on the final assertion itself, not only on the light-map pre-checks.
- What it does not cover: the live `NaturalSpawner` loop (biome lists, pack spawning, player distance). These do not depend on the lamp.
  A live test would be random and need a fake player. The deviation is honest and recorded (plan 8 #4, BACKLOG).

## M20 safety rules (question 5)
- Rule 8: the relaxed "may touch" branch applies only when **both** the new build and the pending one are `kind DEFENCE`. Every pair
  involving a BUILDING still uses M20's grown-by-one test (`mixedPairsKeepTheOneBlockRule` at L1; `wallsQueuedEndToEnd` asserts at L2 that a
  House one block from a pending wall is refused). So house spacing is unchanged.
- FOOTING (11c) only adds refusals, and only for DEFENCE blueprints. `aHouseStillBridgesADip` pins that houses are unchanged.
- `StandPoints.avoiding` now wraps the stand ring for every build. For two houses it is a no-op: grown-by-one boxes leave at least 2 free
  columns between houses, so a ring column is never inside another house's box. For defences it only removes stand points. It cannot let a
  builder stand in a cell that M20 would refuse.
- The UNCHECKED override moved from the enum order to an explicit set holding exactly M20's codes OVERLAP..NO_STAND plus FOOTING. It is
  pinned at L1 (`uncheckedOverrideSetIsExplicit`, `appendedCodesKeepTheOrdinals`). M20's behaviour is unchanged.
- Player blocks, attachments and waterlogged blocks: M21 did not touch `BuildExecutor` or `SiteSupport`. The generated blueprints go
  through the normal parser, so rules 9-11b apply. `defenceAttachmentsSurvive` (lantern and ladders in 4 rotations) and
  `towerCheckPassRepairsLadder` exercise the attachment path.
- **Two builders on touching defences (untested): low to medium risk, not blocking.** Each builder's ring excludes the other pending box,
  so neither stands in the other's cells. A walker crossing the other's cell at a PLACE step hits M20's IN_THE_WAY wait, not a broken
  block. The remaining risks are liveness, not safety: a builder pathing round a long touching L of walls can take long detours, and the
  ring is cached for `RING_TTL`, so a ring computed before the neighbour finished may briefly offer a column that is now cobblestone (the
  walk fails and is recomputed). No path breaks a player block. The BACKLOG line stands. Note 1 below suggests a test.
