verdict: FAIL

The roof-leak fix is correct and its regression tests are mutation-proven. The perf fix is not enough: the first full `scripts/verify.sh` on a clean

## Fix list

1. **Perf test still fails under load in a full verify run (blocking: flakiness and a clean-checkout failure).**
   `src/test/java/dev/villageheart/core/room/RoomScannerPerfTest.java`, `bestBatchMedianNanos`.
   Verify run 1 (11:55Z, load about 3 to 4 on 4 cores): `room16x16x8Under5ms` failed with
   `best batch median 5505 us; batch medians (us): [14799, 14828, 6646, 5505, 6489]`. Uncontended, the same scan takes 1.1 to 1.3 ms (numbers below),
   so this is contention, not the code. **All five batches** were slow because they run back to back and together last well under a second, so one burst
   of contention covers the whole window. Part of that burst comes from the build itself: `gradle.properties` sets `org.gradle.parallel=true`, and in
   `reports/build.log` `:test` runs alongside `:remapJar` and `:compileGametestJava`. Other agents' Minecraft instances add to it. Best-of-5 helps against
   short spikes, but it cannot help against contention that lasts longer than the measurement window.
   Expected (keep the absolute 5 ms limit; do not loosen it):
   - Spread the measurement over a longer wall-clock window. For example, run batches until one batch median is under the limit or a time budget
     (e.g. 5 s, or 50 batches) runs out, with a short pause between batches, and assert on the best. Contention only adds time, so stopping at the first
     batch under the limit is sound, and a real regression still fails because no batch ever gets under it.
   - And/or keep `:test` from overlapping CPU-heavy tasks of the same build (e.g. move the two perf tests to their own `perfTest` task that the other
     tasks `mustRunAfter`, or give them a shared build service with `maxParallelUsages = 1`).
   - Prove it: run the full `scripts/verify.sh` at least twice with other agents' builds running, and also once with a few busy loops on top, and record
     the batch medians.

## Non-blocking notes
1. **Client GameTest crash in verify run 2 (L4), most likely not M4's fault, but please keep an eye on it.** 10 s after the second client world
   loaded (`MarkerClientTests.markerWall`, M3 code), Fabric's client-gametest `NetworkSynchronizer` hit
   `AssertionError: Network synchronizer in invalid state` ("Detected interfacing with packets at a lower level"), and the server crashed. M4 does not
   touch client or client-test code; its only runtime hook is `RoomCells.register()` (two lifecycle listeners that clear a cache). The rerun of Layer 4
   alone passed. It looks like a harness timing flake under load. It was not seen in M1 to M4 r1. If it recurs, it needs its own fix, e.g. finding the
   10 s stall or following Fabric's hint about `fabric.client.gametest.disableNetworkSynchronizer`.
2. The `cell()` bound in T-L1-17 (8 × 4097 = 32 776) is 6.4× the real 5 120 calls, so it cannot catch a doubled `cell()` cost (10 226) or, on its own,
   an unbounded fill of this fixture (10 402). The `openToSky()` bound (4 113 against 4 097) is tight and catches both the unbounded fill and doubled
   sky checks, so the guard as a whole does prove "bounded". Tightening the `cell()` bound to about 2 × 4097 would make it a second, independent
   check. This is the bound the r1 review suggested, so it is not a fault of the builder.
3. r1 notes 2 to 5 still apply unchanged (accepted misreport class C/D, `wallLikeCellsBlock` weak in core, `MarkerView.openToSky`, first real scan
   about 7 ms cold).
