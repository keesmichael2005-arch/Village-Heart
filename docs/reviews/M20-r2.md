## Fix list

1. **Carpets on a cleared cell are not refused and are destroyed** (`build/SiteSupport.ghost`). `CarpetBlock.canSurvive` asks
   `levelReader.isEmptyBlock(below)`. That is a *default* method of `LevelReader`. The proxy handler forwards it with `m.invoke(level, args)`,
   so the default body runs with `this` = the real `ServerLevel`. It reads the real dirt, not the "hole", and the carpet "survives". Probe (white carpet and moss carpet on top
   of the roof-layer dirt of the round-1 layout, Small House `NONE`): `check=OK`, then after `BuildJobs.advance(..., 246)` the carpet is gone.
   The same applies after the confirm, because the per-step check calls the same code. Any default method of `LevelReader`/`BlockGetter` has this fault, so the
   reflective view is only correct for the methods `ServerLevel` implements itself.
   Expected: route default methods back through the proxy, for example `if (m.isDefault()) return InvocationHandler.invokeDefault(proxy, m, args);`
   before the `m.invoke`. I tried exactly that line (then removed it): both carpets are refused (`white_carpet at (...) would fall`) and the
   build stops before them. The other 26 probes give the same answers as before. Add a carpet case to `attachedThingsRefuseTheSite`.
2. **An ascending rail whose raised side rests on a cleared cell is not refused and is destroyed** (`SiteSupport.dependent`). `BaseRailBlock.canSurvive`
   only checks the block below. Vanilla removes an `ASCENDING_*` rail in `neighborChanged` (`shouldBeRemoved`: `!canSupportRigidBlock(level, pos.relative(side))`),
   and `destroyBlock` triggers that. Probe: a rail (and a powered rail) on stone at `west.west()`, shape `ASCENDING_EAST`, against the
   west-face dirt: `check=OK`, and after clearing the rail is gone. Expected: treat a `BaseRailBlock` neighbour whose ascending side is the cleared
   cell as dependent (the ghost already answers `canSupportRigidBlock` for that side), plus a test case.
3. **Farm Plot: the check pass can flood a farmland cell, and the build finishes with 79 farmland and 2 water** (`worker/build/BuilderJob.act`;
   flaky `BuildGameTests.farmPlotBuilt`). Full Layer 2 run 5 of 17 failed:
   `farm_plot_built failed ... 80 farmland and 1 water in plot 0: 79/2`, with `[m20] farm plot finished after 5910 ticks: 121 places, 82 clears`.
   Every other run had 81 clears. Mechanism:
   - Farmland is placed at moisture 7 and dries before the water goes in. A natural run showed moisture 1 or 2 on some cells when the check pass started, and run 14 had one cell re-done (122 places, 82 clears, green).
   - The water is placed before the check pass. The check pass finds `dirt` in a farmland cell (CLEARABLE). `act` clears it in one action and returns.
     The farmland is placed in the *next* action, at least 20 ticks later.
   - If the cell is next to the source, water flows into the hole first. `cellState` then sees water (not CLEARABLE) and returns `ALREADY_RIGHT` for a CHECK
     step, and the build is reported finished with a flooded cell.
   Deterministic repro (temporary GameTest, removed): the real builder, and at the first CHECK step (cursor 404) set farmland (5,0,4) to dirt
   (as a player trampling it would). Result: `farmland=79 water=2 ... water@(5,4) water@(5,5)` every time. A player who tramples the plot during
   the check pass triggers the same in a real world.
   Expected: a cell under a PLACE/CHECK step that holds a natural block is replaced in one action (clear and place together, as `BuildJobs.advance`
   already does), so no fluid can get into the gap. Optionally also keep the farmland from drying (for example, water before the farmland). Add a GameTest
   that sets the farmland next to the water to dirt at the first CHECK step and expects 80 moist farmland and 1 water.

## The reflective proxy

- It is fine for the abstract methods `ServerLevel` implements (`getBlockState`, `getFluidState`), and matching by return type plus a single
  `BlockPos` argument equal to the hole is enough for those.
- It is wrong for every default method (fix 1). `isEmptyBlock` is the one common case among vanilla `canSurvive` implementations:
  `grep` over all `canSurvive` bodies finds only `CarpetBlock` (`isEmptyBlock`), `SporeBlossomBlock` (`isWaterAt`) and `MushroomBlock` (`getRawBrightness`).
  The `invokeDefault` line makes the proxy sound for the whole `LevelReader` surface, so no other approach is needed.
- A `canSurvive` that checks `level instanceof Level` would also behave differently on the proxy. No vanilla one does.
- `canSurvive` does not cover removals that vanilla does in `neighborChanged` (fix 2). Rails are the only common attachable block with that rule.
