## 1. Goal

A cook hired at a Kitchen takes raw food and fuel from the Storage chests, turns it into better food at the Kitchen's smoker
(raw meat and fish → cooked, potato → baked potato) and crafting table (3 wheat → bread, 6 beetroot + a bowl → beetroot soup), and
carries the result back to Storage before dinner. It makes 8 items per in-game hour at level 1, the smoker glows and smokes while it
works, and every item costs fuel (coal, charcoal or logs) from the stockpile. With nothing to burn it stops, and the Villagers tab
says "Needs fuel". Because dinner eats the best food first, a village with a cook eats fewer, better items.

### 3.2 Decisions

1. **Mod-side timed conversion, not the vanilla smoker block entity.** The cook converts items in its own inventory on a `WorkClock`
   while it stands at its smoker; the smoker block only shows it (lit state, vanilla smoke and crackle). Reasons:
   - The vanilla smoker accepts only `minecraft:smoking` recipes: the jar has exactly 9 (`baked_potato`, `cooked_beef`, `cooked_chicken`,
     `cooked_cod`, `cooked_mutton`, `cooked_porkchop`, `cooked_rabbit`, `cooked_salmon`, `dried_kelp` `_from_smoking`) **[verified data
     files]**. Bread (`crafting_shaped`, `###` of wheat) and beetroot soup (`crafting_shapeless`, bowl + 6 beetroot) **[verified]** need a
     crafting step anyway, so half the recipes would be mod-side whatever we chose; one pipeline is simpler to test.
   - Pace: the vanilla smoker cooks one item per 100 ticks, fixed (10 per in-game hour). DESIGN §5.5 wants 8 per hour at level 1 and §4
     wants level and needs multipliers; only a mod clock can do ×1.25/×1.5 (M15) and ×0.5 (M11 hunger).
   - The smoker's three slots are open to players and hoppers; items parked there are neither stockpile nor the cook's, can be taken
     mid-cook, and the furnace stores recipe XP. Mod-side items are always either in Storage or in the cook's inventory.
   - Determinism: counts and fuel use become a pure function of the stock (L1-tested), so the L2 assertions are exact.
   - The visuals come almost free: `SmokerBlock.animateTick` reads `AbstractFurnaceBlock.LIT` **[verified bytecode]** and spawns the smoke
     particles and the crackle sound on the client. `AbstractFurnaceBlockEntity.serverTick` writes `LIT` only when its own `isLit()`
     changed during the tick (it compares the value at the start of the tick with the value at the end, then `setBlock(pos, state, 3)`)
     **[verified bytecode]**, so a `LIT = true` that we set on an idle smoker stays. A player can still use the smoker by hand; vanilla
     then drives `LIT` while it burns, and the cook sets it again for its next item.
2. **Recipes** (core `CookRecipes`, a fixed table, not read from the `RecipeManager`: DESIGN pins these four families, data packs must
   not change what villagers do, and a table is unit-testable):

   | Kind | Input → output (per output item) | Station |
   |---|---|---|
   | SMOKE | beef → cooked_beef, porkchop → cooked_porkchop, chicken → cooked_chicken, mutton → cooked_mutton, rabbit → cooked_rabbit, cod → cooked_cod, salmon → cooked_salmon, potato → baked_potato | smoker |
   | CRAFT | 3 wheat → bread; 6 beetroot + 1 bowl → beetroot_soup | crafting table, then smoker |

   All ids `minecraft:`. The 8 SMOKE inputs are M11's raw list (A4). Kelp is not food and is left out. Inputs and fuel are only
   **component-free** stacks (`StackKey.patch().isEmpty()`): a renamed beef or a named log is a player's item and is never taken (it is
   still eaten at dinner by M11's rules).
3. **Every recipe needs fuel**, CRAFT included (bread is baked and soup simmered on the Kitchen's fire). This is how the roadmap's
   "no fuel → nothing is cooked" holds for its own setup (beef *and* wheat), and it keeps one rule for the "Needs fuel" status.
4. **Fuel as heat.** Core `FuelTable`: `minecraft:coal` 1600, `minecraft:charcoal` 1600, every item in `#minecraft:logs_that_burn`
   (oak, spruce, birch, jungle, acacia, dark oak, pale oak, mangrove, cherry logs and woods, stripped or not) 300 **[verified tag file]**;
   these equal vanilla's furnace burn times (`level.fuelValues().burnDuration(stack)`, **[verified]** `Level.fuelValues()` /
   `FuelValues.burnDuration(ItemStack)`; T-L2-9 compares them). One output item costs `HEAT_PER_ITEM` = 200, the furnace's 200-tick
   cook time (the smoker halves both burn and cook time, `SmokerBlockEntity.getBurnDuration` = super / 2 **[verified]**, so the ratio
   is vanilla's either way): coal or charcoal = 8 items, a log = 1.5. Nether stems, planks, sticks and lava are not fuel (DESIGN names
   coal, charcoal and logs). Each **smoker** (job block) has a saved **heat bank** (int, `VillageState.smokerHeat`, 3.7; per smoker, not per Kitchen, so two cooks never plan against the same heat): when the cook is at its
   smoker and the bank holds < 200 for the next unit, it "loads" one carried fuel item (it leaves the inventory, heat += burn), so unused
   fuel stays carried and goes back to Storage with the batch's leftovers; each finished item subtracts 200. The bank never goes
   negative, and a part-used coal is never lost on a restart or while the Kitchen is briefly invalid (3.4 `KitchenSystem`). **Fuel order**: charcoal, coal, then logs by id (coal before logs protects
   building wood; tunable list in `Balance.COOK_FUEL_ORDER`).
5. **Pace**: `WorkClock(COOK_TICKS_PER_ITEM = 125)` (1000 ticks per in-game hour / 8) ticked with `WorkSpeed.untooled(level, needs)`
   (A1): 125 ticks per item at level 1 fed and housed; ×0.5 hungry → 250; level 2 → 100 (M15). One unit = one output item (a loaf, not
   3 wheat). The clock runs only while the cook is within `COOK_REACH` = 2.0 of its smoker and the bank holds ≥ 200; the remainder is
   kept per worker in `CookRuntime` (not in the behaviour), so a behaviour restart does not shift the gaps.
6. **Batches.** The cook plans one recipe at a time (`CookPlanner`, pure). Recipe choice: highest **gain** first, gain = output nutrition −
   nutrition of the inputs (vanilla values pinned in `CookRecipes`, checked against `DataComponents.FOOD` in T-L2-9): beef, porkchop 8−3 = 5;
   bread 5−0 = 5; mutton, chicken, salmon 6−2 = 4; potato 5−1 = 4; cod 5−2 = 3; rabbit 5−3 = 2; soup 6−6 = 0 (still worth it: one item
   instead of six, and §5.5 asks for it). Ties: output nutrition descending, then output id. Batch size
   `n = min(COOK_BATCH = 8, inputs available / per-output input, units the time allows, units the fuel allows)`:
   - inputs available = `Stockpile.available(key, cook)` (stock minus *other* holders' reservations) + what the cook already carries;
   - time: work must end by `COOK_DONE_BY` = `WORK_END` − 500 = 10500 so the food is in Storage before dinner at 11000:
     `max(0, floor((COOK_DONE_BY − dayTime − walk) × speed / 125))`, with `walk` = the estimated legs of this batch (smoker → nearest
     holding Storage → smoker, plus the crafting table for CRAFT, plus the deposit leg) × `WALK_TICKS_PER_BLOCK` 10 per block of straight
     distance + `COOK_WALK_SLACK` 100; 0 → no new batch. After `COOK_DONE_BY` the cook stops cooking even with inputs in hand (planner
     row 4 is time-gated) and hands everything back (`returning`), so nothing is in its pockets at 11000;
   - slots: inputs, one fuel slot and the outputs must fit the villager's 8 slots: `n ≤ (free slots − input slots − 1) × output max
     stack`, so a soup batch (beetroot soup does not stack) is at most 5 with an empty inventory;
   - fuel: `(bank + fuel available × burn) / 200` with one fuel kind, the first in the fuel order that covers the whole batch, else the one
     that covers the most (the batch shrinks to it). Fuel to fetch `k = ceil(max(0, n × 200 − bank) / burn)`.
   Worked example (the roadmap L2, pinned in T-L1-4): 10 beef, 9 wheat, 4 coal, bank 0 → batch 1 beef × 8, 1 coal (bank 1600 → 0);
   batch 2 beef × 2, 1 coal (bank 1600 → 1200); batch 3 bread × 3, no coal (bank 1200 → 600). Result: 10 cooked beef, 3 bread, coal 4 → 2,
   bank 600.
7. **Withdraw with reservations.** When the planner picks `FETCH(batch)`, the glue first `release(uuid)`s anything the cook still holds, then
   reserves the batch's inputs and fuel under the cook's UUID with `set(uuid, key, min(n, available(key, uuid)))` in the same tick (the
   server thread is single-threaded, so a second cook planning in that tick sees the first one's claim) and recomputes the batch from
   what was actually reserved; the planner is not consulted again until the fetch ends, so dinner (A4), a second cook and a later builder (M20) cannot count on
   them. The cook walks (`WalkTo`, within 2.0 of the `StorageSite.anchor`) to the nearest Storage **holding** each key in turn (new M6 API
   `Stockpile.nearestHolding(from, key, holder)` = nearest site with ≥ 1 *available to that holder*; glue
   `Stockpiles.nearestHolding(level, from, key, holder) : Optional<BlockPos>`; one stop per (key, site), at most 6) and withdraws from
   **that site only** (new `Stockpiles.withdrawAt(level, anchor, key, n, holder)` over `Stockpile.withdrawFrom(site, …)`, consuming its
   reservation); what is short there is looked for at the next nearest site. Reservations are released
   (`release(uuid)`) when the fetch ends (complete or short), after a fetch timeout of 400 + 20 × (sum of the straight leg lengths) ticks, when the behaviour stops (WORK
   ends, panic), on fire, death or unload (`CookRuntime.forget`), and implicitly on a restart (M6 reservations are transient). A short
   withdraw (a player took the items meanwhile) shrinks the batch to what the cook holds; fuel that came anyway is loaded into the bank.
8. **Outputs back to Storage.** Cooked items go into the cook's inventory as each unit completes and are **kept** (excluded from the
   deposit load) until the batch is done, so neither M9's `wanted` nor the planner's YIELD row sends the cook to Storage after every
   item. A unit completes only if its output fits in the inventory; nothing is ever popped into the world (vanilla villagers would pick
   bread up). When the batch is done (or nothing more can be made from what it holds), `JobBehaviours.depositNow(cook)` becomes true and M7's `DepositTrip` carries everything not kept to the
   nearest Storage with space (same trip, same `STORAGE_FULL` rules). At GO_HOME (11000) the cook keeps nothing: leftover inputs and fuel
   go back too. Planner order puts YIELD before FETCH, so the cook always empties its hands before it fetches the next batch.
9. **Visual feedback** (glue `KitchenVisuals`): while the clock runs at its smoker, the cook sets `LIT = true` on that smoker
   (`level.setBlock(pos, state.setValue(AbstractFurnaceBlock.LIT, true), 3)` **[verified `LIT` is a public static `BooleanProperty`]**) once
   per unit (only if it is not lit already, so a vanilla burn that ends mid-batch does not leave it dark), looks at it (`LOOK_TARGET` = `new BlockPosTracker(pos)` **[verified ctor]**) and swings its arm when an item is done
   (`LivingEntity.swing(InteractionHand.MAIN_HAND)` **[verified]**). For CRAFT batches it first walks to the Kitchen's crafting table
   (the scan's `minecraft:crafting_table` cell nearest the smoker), stands there `COOK_PREP_TICKS` = 20 swinging once (the "prep"), then
   goes to the smoker; a table it cannot reach within 200 ticks is skipped (counter `work.cook.prep_skipped`, never blocks the batch).
   It unlights the smoker when the batch ends, the behaviour stops, or the cook leaves reach. Clean-up for the cases the behaviour never
   sees (crash, reload, fire while unloaded): `KitchenSystem` every 100 ticks turns `LIT` off on every smoker of a valid Kitchen's scan
   (loaded chunks only) that is lit, has no cook currently cooking at it (`CookRuntime`), and whose block entity is not burning by itself.
   The burning check needs `AbstractFurnaceBlockEntity.litTimeRemaining`, which is package-private (`isLit()` is private) **[verified]**:
   one accessor mixin `AbstractFurnaceBlockEntityAccessor` (`@Accessor("litTimeRemaining") int villageheart$litTimeRemaining()`), added
   to `villageheart.mixins.json`. LIT toggles at most twice per batch (light level changes are cheap at that rate); the M5 building
   checks are round-robin scans, not block-change listeners, so a toggle never triggers a rescan.
10. **Status.** `WorkerStatus.NEEDS_FUEL` ("Needs fuel", lang `status.villageheart.needs_fuel`): the cook has something to cook (inputs in
    stock or in hand) but the bank is below 200 and no fuel is available in the stockpile. Nothing to cook → `NO_WORK`
    (`status.villageheart.no_work.cook` = "Nothing to cook"), whatever the fuel. Fuel in the Kitchen chest does **not** count: DESIGN §5.5
    says fuel comes from the stockpile, and the Kitchen chest is not stockpile (M6 rule). With NEEDS_FUEL the cook hands back any inputs
    it carries (`returning` flag, keep = 0) so raw food is not stuck in its pockets at dinner. The status clears at the first planner pass
    that finds fuel (≤ 20 ticks).
11. **Where the cook stands.** Its own job block (`WorkerData.jobBlock`, one cook per smoker, M7 `JobSlots`); two cooks in one Kitchen
    share nothing but the stockpile (each smoker has its own heat bank).
12. **No tool.** `JobType.COOK.tool()` stays empty (DESIGN §5.5 names none). The Kitchen chest is unused by M13 (later: M15 levels,
    player-set recipe priorities; out of scope).

  for `WALK_TARGET`; entry `WALK_TARGET` REGISTERED, `LOOK_TARGET` REGISTERED; long-running like
  M9's `LumberjackWork`). Each tick inside `VillageTicker.section()`: re-plan every 20 ticks or when the action ends (never in the middle of a FETCH or a unit), then run it:
  - `FETCH`: reserve (3.2 #7), `WalkTo` each Storage anchor, withdraw, release; then CRAFT prep (3.2 #9); then to the smoker.
  - `COOK`: `WalkTo(smoker, closeEnough 1)`; in `COOK_REACH`: load carried fuel into the bank (`VillageSavedData.update`) only as much as the
    remaining units need (the rest stays carried and kept), light the smoker, `WorkClock.tick(speed)`; per completed unit: re-check the
    inputs are still in the inventory, remove them, add the output (`SimpleContainer.canAddItem` first; no room → the batch ends early;
    nothing is ever popped into the world), bank −= 200, swing, `WorkStats.add(cook,
    items, 1)` and `add(cook, <output path>, 1)`, `LAST_WORKED_AT_POI` stamp.
  - `IDLE`: `WalkTo(smoker)` and stand (replaces M7's `GoToJobBlock` for the cook, which would fight `WalkTo`).
  - `stop()`: unlight, release reservations, keep the clock remainder.
- `JobBehaviours.work(v)` for COOK = `[DepositTrip (1), CookWork (3)]` (no `GoToJobBlock` or `StandAtJobBlock`: `CookWork` walks to the
  smoker itself and stamps `LAST_WORKED_AT_POI`); `goHome` unchanged.
- `CookRuntime` (per server, cleared on claim change): per cook {clock, current batch, `returning`, fetch stops + deadline, smoker lit,
  unit log for tests}; `cooking(smokerPos)` for `KitchenSystem`.
- `KitchenSystem` (`villageheart:kitchen`, every 100 ticks, timed section): drop a smoker's heat bank only when its block is no
  longer a smoker (loaded chunk) or its Kitchen is unregistered (marker gone); a briefly invalid Kitchen keeps it, unlight stray lit smokers (3.2 #9).
- `CookCatalog`: `StackKey` → recipe/fuel role: component-free, id lookup in `CookRecipes`, `#minecraft:logs_that_burn` via `StackKey.is`.

### 3.5 Keep and deposit rules (changes to M7/M9/M10 code)

- `JobBehaviours.keep` COOK branch = `CookKeep.keep`: during WORK and not `returning`, keep the inputs, the fuel and the outputs made so far of
  the current batch (after a reload, with no batch in the runtime: every carried input that forms whole units and every fuel item, so planner row 4
  resumes); keep 0 of everything else (the outputs). At GO_HOME, or when `returning`: keep 0.
- **The FoodKeep top-up does not apply to COOK** (A3): a cook's food is its product. One `if (job != COOK)` in M10's `DepositTrip` path.
- `JobBehaviours.depositNow(v)` COOK = carries anything with keep < count (by the rule above: the batch is done, or `returning`, or
  something foreign is carried). The planner gets the same predicate as `depositNow` (row 3), so nothing is circular.
- Vanilla breeding (`VillagerMakeLove` in M7's IDLE, WAKE phase only) eats bread, potatoes and beetroot from villager inventories; the
  cook carries none outside WORK (GO_HOME keep 0), so its products are not eaten that way.
  `DepositTrip`'s start condition becomes `wanted(v) || depositNow(v)` (with load > 0); everything else in the trip is unchanged.

### 3.9 The L3 comparison and how the baseline is captured

**Scenario** `sim/scenarios/starter_cook.json` = M11's `starter.json` actions, unchanged, plus:
```json
{ "action": "field", "at": [10, 23], "size": 9, "crop": "wheat", "age": 7 },
{ "action": "hire", "day": 1, "tick": 200, "job": "cook", "building": "kitchen" }
```
and `"minecraft:coal": 32` added to the Storage prefab's `chest_items` (256 items of heat for 10 days, about twice the expected use, so the lumberjack's logs are not
burnt and `starter`'s "logs up every day" assertion stays meaningful; T-L2-3 covers log fuel). Why a wheat field: carrots (3) and the kit
bread are already "prepared" food, so a cook in plain `starter` has nothing to cook and the average cannot move. **Wheat is not food**, so
without a cook the extra field adds nothing to what is eaten: the eaten stream of `starter_cook` minus its cook is the same carrots + kit
bread + apples as `starter`. That is why `starter` (M11) is a fair baseline even though the layouts differ by one field; the optional
control run of 5.4 measures it directly if a result is ever doubted. The field sits beside the carrot field (centres ≈ 15 blocks from
the composter, inside M10's 24) and away from the grove; the builder may move it by a few blocks if T-L2-11 shows a clash (deviation).

**Baseline file.** `reports/` is git-ignored, so the baseline is committed separately: `sim/baselines/starter.json` = the complete report of
one PASSing `scripts/simulate.sh starter` run (10 days) made on the M13 branch (the cook code is idle in `starter`, so this is the M11
behaviour; it also proves M13 did not break `starter`). Capturing it is step 10a. Its value for the comparison is
**B = Σ food.eaten_nutrition / Σ food.eaten_items over days 3..10** (days 1-2 are the kit bread in both runs and only dilute the signal).
The builder writes B, and M11's PROGRESS number if one exists, into PROGRESS (if they differ by > 0.3, record both and investigate before
step 10b).

**Grammar** (M2 extension, `core.sim.Assertion`; `+ - * /` with the usual precedence and "division by zero → FAIL with a reason" are
already in M2's parser and evaluator, so only `@base` is new): a ref may carry `@base` **inside a function** (the only `@` allowed there); it reads the
baseline table at the same day. `Scenario` gains an optional `baseline` (a path relative to the scenario file, here
`"../baselines/starter.json"`), parsed by `ScenarioParser`; `core.sim.BaselineLoader.load(path) : MetricsTable` (pure, via
`MetricsJson.read`). Evaluation without a baseline, or with a scope outside its days, is a FAIL with a reason (never a silent pass).
The report gains `baseline_file` and `scenario_sha256` (SHA-256 of the scenario's canonical JSON: parsed with `core.sim.Json`, re-written with sorted keys and no whitespace, so line endings
and formatting do not matter) and `git_commit` (from `SIM_GIT_COMMIT`, set by `simulate.sh`; informational).

**Assertions** of `starter_cook` = all of `starter`'s, with `employed == 3 on day 1`, plus:
```
"sum(work.cook.items) >= 40 from day 2",
"sum(food.eaten_nutrition) / sum(food.eaten_items) > sum(food.eaten_nutrition@base) / sum(food.eaten_items@base) from day 3"
```
The first is a guard (the cook really worked; expected ≈ 10 loaves a day); the second is the roadmap criterion. Its result text in the
report JSON holds both numbers (`lhs=… > rhs=…`, M2 format), which is "reported in the JSON".

**Staleness guard** (L1 `ScenarioFilesTest`): the baseline report's `scenario` is `starter`, `status` PASS, ≥ 10 days, Σ eaten items on
days 3..10 > 0, and its `scenario_sha256` equals the SHA-256 of today's `sim/scenarios/starter.json`. Changing `starter.json` without
re-capturing the baseline fails verify. Code changes (farmer, dinner, needs) cannot be hashed, so TESTING.md gets the rule: a change to `starter.json`, `DinnerPlanner`,
`FoodRules` or M10's harvest rules re-captures the baseline in the same commit; `simulate.sh` prints a WARN when the baseline's
`git_commit` is more than 50 commits behind HEAD. Running both scenarios in one invocation was considered and rejected: it doubles
every `starter_cook` run (≈ 20 min) for a check that matters only when those files change.

Expected size of the effect (so the builder knows what "rises" should look like): 8-12 villagers; days 3-10 baseline ≈ carrots (3) with
greedy 4-carrot meals plus a few apples → B ≈ 3.1-3.4. With the cook, the wheat field (≈ 80 plants, ≈ 2.4 days per cycle, 1 wheat each)
gives ≈ 11 bread a day ≈ 5 adult meals at 5 each → ≈ 3.8-4.1. A margin of ≈ 0.5 against a run-to-run spread we expect well under 0.2.

## 6. Risks & unknowns, out of scope

**Risks** (highest first):
1. **(top) L3 depends on three unbuilt jobs and one unseeded comparison.** `starter_cook` needs M10's farmer to harvest two 9×9 fields
   and deposit the wheat during the day, M9's lumberjack to keep logs rising, M11's dinner, and the cook; and it compares one run with
   one baseline run. Mitigations: the comparison is over days 3-10 sums (not a day), the expected gap (≈ 0.5) is several times the
   expected spread, the guard assertion makes "cook idle" fail loudly, T-L2-11 checks the layout in verify, and the optional control
   run (T-L3-2) isolates the field. If T-L3-1 fails, the builder reports per-day ratios, cooked items and statuses and does **not**
   loosen an assertion (CLAUDE.md rule 4).
2. **Farmer wheat flow timing.** M7's deposit fires at one full stack or at GO_HOME (11000, the dinner tick). Wheat harvested late
   reaches Storage after dinner and is cooked the next day; this only delays food by a day (M11 risk 2). Not changed here.
3. **Assumption drift** (3.1): `DepositTrip` hooks (`wanted`, `keep`, FoodKeep) are M9/M10 code not yet written. M13 adds one OR and one
   exemption; if their shape differs, step 5 adapts and records it.
4. **LIT handling** relies on the verified `serverTick` rule; a mod or data pack that ticks smokers differently could flicker the light.
   The clean-up sweep bounds a stray lit smoker to 100 ticks.
5. **Fuel competition later**: M19's blacksmith will burn the same coal. The fuel order and reservations already prevent double counting;
   priorities between jobs are M19's/M26's.

**Out of scope**: other foods (stews, cake, cookies, pumpkin pie, honey, kelp), player-set recipe priorities or a "keep raw" switch, the
Kitchen chest as a source, a fuel reserve for building logs (tunable later: `COOK_FUEL_ORDER` can drop logs), XP and levels (M15 reads
`WorkClock` units), held-item visuals (M9 reason: no safe hand copy), what a fired cook carries (same open question for every job: it
stays in the villager's inventory; a shared "hand back on fire" rule belongs to M8/M24), the Heart screen (M8), merchant sales of
cooked food (M22).
