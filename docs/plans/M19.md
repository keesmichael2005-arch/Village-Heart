## 1. Goal

A blacksmith hired at a Town-tier Smithy smelts raw iron, copper and gold from the stockpile into ingots at its blast furnace (with
fuel). It **makes the tools and gear the village's workers ask for, and nothing else**: when a hired lumberjack, miner, hunter or farmer
has no usable tool in its job chest (or its tool is about to break), or a guard lacks a sword or an armour piece, the smith makes that one
item from the **best material the stockpile can afford right now** (wood, stone, copper, iron, diamond under a reserve rule), which takes
a long time (a stone tool ≈ 1200 ticks, an iron one a quarter of its day, a diamond one half), and carries it to the worker's chest. A tool
that still works is never replaced, so the village climbs the tiers as tools wear out; a tool the player puts in a chest is used at once
and cancels the smith's order. It repairs damaged tools and armour left in the Smithy chest (and the Barracks chest) with ingots. It builds iron golems from 36 ingots and a carved pumpkin over one working day (at most 1 golem per 5 villagers), and a
golem below half health walks to the Smithy and is patched up with ingots (25 HP each). Villagers inside the claim no longer summon
golems themselves: golems come from the Smithy.

### 3.2 Decisions

1. **Everything the blacksmith makes is a mod-side timed conversion, as in M13** (decision 1 there): items move between the stockpile and
   the smith's inventory, a `WorkClock` turns inputs into outputs while the smith stands at its station, and the vanilla blocks only show
   it. No crafting grid, no `RecipeManager`, no block-entity slots: DESIGN pins the recipes, data packs must not change villagers, the
   table is L1-testable, and nothing ever sits in a furnace slot where a player or hopper can take it mid-job.
   Stations: **smelting** at the job block (blast furnace, `LIT` on while the clock runs); **crafting, gear repair and golem work** at the
   Smithy's anvil (the scan's `#anvil` cell nearest the blast furnace), with the anvil sound `level.levelEvent(LevelEvent.SOUND_ANVIL_USED
   = 1030, anvilPos, 0)` **[verified constant, `LevelAccessor.levelEvent(int, BlockPos, int)` verified]** and `swing(MAIN_HAND)`
   **[verified]** on each finished unit. Golem work also lights the blast furnace (see #9).
2. **Recipes** (core `SmithRecipes`, all ids `minecraft:`, counts **[verified data files]**):

   | Kind | Output ← inputs | Ticks per unit (level 1) |
   |---|---|---|
   | SMELT | iron_ingot ← raw_iron; copper_ingot ← raw_copper; gold_ingot ← raw_gold | 100 (the blast furnace's `cookingtime` 100 **[verified]**) |
   | CRAFT tool, 5 tiers × 4 kinds | `<tier>_axe`, `<tier>_pickaxe` ← 3 material + 2 stick; `<tier>_hoe` ← 2 + 2; `<tier>_sword` ← 2 + 1 stick, for tier prefix `wooden` / `stone` / `copper` / `iron` / `diamond` **[verified: the 30 recipes `minecraft:{wooden,stone,copper,golden,iron,diamond}_{axe,pickaxe,hoe,shovel,sword,spear}` all exist in the 1.21.11 jar with these counts]** | by tier, `SMITH_TOOL_TICKS` *(tunable)*: wooden 600, stone 1200, copper 1800, iron 2400, diamond 4800 |
   | CRAFT armour, 4 tiers × 4 pieces | `<tier>_helmet` 5, `_chestplate` 8, `_leggings` 7, `_boots` 4 material, for `leather` / `copper` / `iron` / `diamond` **[verified recipes; no chainmail recipe exists]** | by tier, `SMITH_ARMOUR_TICKS` *(tunable)*: leather 600, copper 1800, iron 2400, diamond 4800 per piece |
   | STICKS | 4 stick ← 2 of any `#minecraft:planks` (`minecraft:stick` **[verified]**; made only as a step of a tool order when the stockpile has too few sticks) | 20 |
   | GOLEM | 1 iron golem ← 36 iron_ingot + 1 carved_pumpkin | `GOLEM_BUILD_TICKS` 8000 (3.2 #9) |

   **Materials per tier** (the vanilla item tags, read in the 1.21.11 data **[verified]**): wooden = `#minecraft:wooden_tool_materials`
   = `#minecraft:planks` (any planks, mixed kinds allowed: the smith counts the tag, not one id); stone = `#minecraft:stone_tool_materials`
   = cobblestone, blackstone, cobbled_deepslate (mixed allowed, as in vanilla crafting); copper = `#copper_tool_materials` = copper_ingot;
   iron = `#iron_tool_materials` = iron_ingot; diamond = `#diamond_tool_materials` = diamond. The glue reads the tags (`StackKey.is`), the
   core gets the count per tier material. **Gold**: the `golden_*` recipes exist but the smith **does not make gold tools or armour** in
   v1: gold has 32 durability (iron 250), so 3 gold ingots buy 3 miner hauls; with A9 a gold tool would be the fastest tool in the game
   (×6.0), which makes it a trap for the planner, not a choice. Gold tools the player puts in a chest are used (they satisfy a demand).
   **Netherite**: upgrade-only (`minecraft:netherite_*_smithing`, `smithing_transform` of a diamond item + `netherite_upgrade_smithing_template`
   + `#netherite_tool_materials` **[verified]**): not made by the smith in v1 (a Nether template and netherite ingots are a late-game
   player thing); a player's netherite tool is used. **Shovels and spears** (both exist in 1.21.11, shovel 1 + 2, spear 1 + 2) are **not** in `SmithRecipes`: no job asks for them in v1
   (critique r2 #22); T-L2-10 checks the 20 tool and 16 armour recipes the smith does make. **Why so slow** (owner, 2026-11-09): the recipe counts stay vanilla because
   the player can hand-craft the same tool at any time; the smith's limiter is **time** (wooden tool 600 ticks ≈ 6 % of its ~10 000-tick
   work day, iron 24 %, diamond 48 %), scaled by `WorkSpeed.untooled(level, needs)` (level 3 makes an iron tool in 1600 ticks).

   Only component-free input stacks are taken (`StackKey.patch().isEmpty()`), as in M13. Smelting needs fuel (M13's heat bank, 200
   heat per ingot, so a coal smelts 8: the blast furnace halves both burn and cook time, the ratio is vanilla's); crafting, sticks,
   repair and golem work need none (DESIGN §5.7 names fuel for smelting only). Ore blocks and raw-ore blocks are not smelted in v1.
3. **Smelting = M13's cook pipeline with another recipe table.** Batch of at most `SMITH_BATCH` 8 (`BatchMath` units by input, time,
   slots and fuel), fetch with reservations (`release` → `set(uuid, key, n)` → recompute; `nearestHolding` + `withdrawAt`), load fuel
   into the **blast furnace's** heat bank, clock 100 ticks per ingot × `WorkSpeed.untooled`, outputs kept until the batch ends, then
   `depositNow` → `DepositTrip`. Fuel order: charcoal, coal, logs (M13's `COOK_FUEL_ORDER`, shared as `FUEL_ORDER`). **Fuel competition
   with the cook** (M13 risk 5): when the village has a hired cook, the smith leaves `SMITH_FUEL_RESERVE` = 3200 heat (2 coal) of fuel
   in the stockpile for it (pure: `available heat − reserve`, L1). `NEEDS_FUEL` as in M13: raw ore to smelt, nothing else to do, no fuel.
4. **Gear demand planner (pure core, the roadmap's L1; r2 replaces r1's gear targets and spare stock).** The smith makes a tool or
   armour piece **only for an actual demand** of a hired worker (owner, 2026-11-09). There is no spare stock, nothing for unhired jobs and
   nothing for a job whose building is not registered.
   - **Demand slots, counted per camp** (critique r2 #2): demand is a **count per (building, need)**, not per worker, because the tools
     sit in a chest the camp's workers share and M9's `ToolAllocation.assign` hands them out best first in hiring order. Where a worker's
     tool is while it works is **verified on dev** (DESIGN 5.1/5.3/5.4 "Rules as built"; A11): it stays in the camp chest (M9 "tools stay
     in the camp chest", M16 "the pickaxe stays in the Mine chest", M17 "the weapon stays in the Lodge chest"), so the chest is the whole
     picture. Built by the glue `DemandCensus` every 100 ticks and directly before an order starts; **r3: the tool-worker part is M19a's
     `ToolCensus.of` / `ToolDemand.of` per camp, unchanged** (`DemandCensus` only loops over the tool buildings, adds the guards and
     applies "one stack, one demand"):
     - *Tool workers*: lumberjack → AXE, miner → PICKAXE, hunter → SWORD (its `BOW_OR_SWORD` need; a bow needs string the village does not
       make, so the smith answers with a sword), farmer → HOE. For each registered building of that job: `hired` = its hired workers,
       `fresh` = the not-worn tools of the kind in its camp containers (`CampContainers.of`, `CampTools.isTool`). `needed = hired − fresh`
       (≥ 0). Slots `0..needed−1` are demand slots `OrderKey(building, need, slot)`. A slot is **MISSING** when `hired − (fresh + worn)
       > slot` (some worker has no tool at all: it has stopped, "Needs axe"; the farmer still works at ×1.0), else **WORN**. The `current`
       rank of a WORN slot comes from a full `ToolAllocation.assign` over all tools (worn included, as M9's lease hands them out): the rank
       of the worn tool of the worker that slot stands for (in hiring order from the back). (All of this is M19a's `ToolDemand.of`; the
       slot index is only an identifier, and presence is counted **per camp**: see "One order per slot" below.)
     - *Worn* (M19a's `ToolWear.worn(remaining, max, wearPerUnit, unitTicks, leadTicks)`, critique r2 #14): `remaining <
       max(ceil(GEAR_MIN_DURABILITY × max), 2 × wearPerUnit, wearPerUnit × ceil(leadTicks / unitTicks))`. **The smith's lead is
       `leadTicks = craftTicks + 600`**: the replacement's craft (the worn tool's own tier, level 1, `SMITH_TOOL_TICKS`) plus a delivery
       allowance (M19a's self-craft passes 0). **r3: `unitTicks` follows the worn tool's tier** for the tier-paced jobs (tool-speed is
       merged): M19 adds `WearProfile.unitTicksAt(ToolTier t)` = `ceil(unitTicks × TOOL_SPEED[STONE] / TOOL_SPEED[t])` for lumberjack and
       miner, `unitTicks` unchanged for hunter and farmer (their pace ignores the tier); with lead 0 (self-craft) the result does not
       depend on it, so M19a's lines are unchanged. Wear per unit: lumberjack 1 (a log; 40 ticks at stone, 27 at iron, 80 at wood), farmer 1
       (a harvest, 20), miner 10 (`MINER_WEAR_PER_HAUL`, a haul 2000 at stone, 1334 at iron, 4000 at wood), hunter 5
       (`HUNTER_WEAR_PER_TRIP`, a trip ≈ 3000). Examples (pinned in T-L1-2b): stone axe 131 → worn below 45 (1800 / 40 logs; unchanged);
       wooden pickaxe 59 → below 20 (two hauls; 1200 / 4000 is one); iron pickaxe 250 → **below 30** (3000 / 1334 → 3 hauls; r2 said 25
       with the old 2000-tick haul); iron axe 250 → **below 112** (3000 / 27 logs; r2 said 75 at 40 ticks per log); wooden axe 59 → below
       15 (1200 / 80). *(tunable; M26 may move the allowance.)*
     - **One worn line for everyone while a smith serves** (r3, M19a hand-off rule 4): with `SmithService.servesNow` true, M19a's three
       `ToolCensus.of(..., 0)` call sites (`SelfCraftJob` twice, `SelfCraftSystem`) take the lead from the new shared `GearLead.ticks(server,
       village)` (0 without a serving smith, else the smith's per-tool lead above), so the self-craft cancel check and the smith's census

     - *Guards* (M18; critique r2 #9, #10, #24): for guard g = 1..guards and slot in [WEAPON (sword), CHEST, LEGS, HEAD, FEET]: count the
       usable pieces **only where guards draw gear**: worn by loaded guards, in Barracks chests, in the stockpile, and pieces a smith carries

     - **One stack, one demand** (critique r2 #9): in one census pass each physical stack covers at most one slot; tool workers are served
       first, then guards; `FetchAndDeliver` takes its stack from the same allocation.
     - **What satisfies a demand** (the player's shortcut, by design): any not-worn tool of the kind in the job chest, whatever its tier
       and whoever put it there, including gold and netherite (owner: "the smith is automation, not the only source").
     - **Three-valued census** (critique r2 #1): a camp whose chests cannot be read (no scan, unloaded, `CampTools.Missing.UNKNOWN`) or
       whose building is registered but **invalid** yields `UNKNOWN` for its slots: no new order, but an open order there is **paused**,
       never cancelled.
   - **Urgency classes** (first class first; within a class in building order, then slot): **U1** a MISSING slot of a REQUIRED tool
     (lumberjack, miner, hunter: a worker cannot work); **U2** a guard's missing sword; **U3** a guard's missing armour piece (CHEST, LEGS,
     HEAD, FEET); **U4** WORN slots of REQUIRED-tool jobs; **U5** the farmer's hoe (BONUS tool: MISSING or WORN).
   - **One order per slot**: an order (`GearOrder(demand, recipe, plan, prerequisites)`) holds its `OrderKey`; an open key (fetching,
     crafting, finished and carried) gets no second order; two smiths never take the same key, and the smith never takes a key a
     self-crafter holds nor the reverse (M19a hand-off rule 3). **r3: the registry is M19a's `GearOrders`** (holder = the smith's UUID),
     not a separate `SmithOrders`; it is rebuilt after a restart from the saved `self_crafts` **and** `smith_crafts` and from finished
     tools the smiths carry (3.4). **Orders are counted per camp, not per slot index** (M19a critique #3): a camp (building, need) can take
     a new smith order only while its open orders (self-crafts and smith orders together) are fewer than `needed`, and the new order takes
     the lowest slot index no open order uses (`ToolDemand.freeSlots(CampDemand, Set<OrderKey> openOfCamp)`, added by M19 beside M19a's
     per-worker `slotFor`). When `needed` drops below the open orders of a (building, need), M19a's `GearDemandPlanner.surplus(openOfCamp,
     needed)` picks the one with the **least progress** (critique r2 #2), whoever holds it; its holder cancels it.
   - **Tier choice** (`TierChoice.best(demand, materials, smeltable, gear, settings)`, added by M19 to M19a's `TierChoice` beside
     `selfMade`; "quality follows resources"): the **best tier the stockpile
     can afford at that moment** in the order DIAMOND > IRON > COPPER > STONE > WOODEN (tools) or DIAMOND > IRON > COPPER > LEATHER (armour).
     "Afford" = `available (count − reserved by others)` (the smith's own reservations for another job do not count, critique r2 #25)
     `+ carried for this order ≥ cost`, and for tools also sticks ≥ cost or planks to make them (the STICKS prerequisite, 2 planks → 4
     sticks; for a wooden tool the planks must cover head **and** sticks). Ingots count **plus the raw ore the smith can smelt now**
     (raw ore available and fuel for it after the cook reserve of 3.2 #3): the order then has a SMELT prerequisite (n raw → n ingots,
     100 ticks each), so a miner is not given a stone pick while raw iron sits in Storage. **r3, reuse**: the wooden and stone candidates
     are planned by M19a's own plan builder (the one behind `selfMade`, made package-visible as `TierChoice.plan(ToolRecipe, Materials)`
     and generalised from "planks or stone head" to `CraftTier.headRole()`), so the smith and a self-crafter pick materials, stick
     batches and leftovers the same way (per concrete `StackKey`, M19a critique #7); copper/iron/diamond/leather use the same builder with
     their head role. **Logs are not a smith material**: the smith's `Materials` has no `LOGS` items (logs are its smelting fuel, 3.2 #3),
     so its wooden plans use planks only (a self-crafter may still turn logs into planks).
     Then:
     - **Never a proactive upgrade** (owner, 2026-11-09): a not-worn tool is never replaced because better material appeared; quality
       rises through wear.
     - **Replacement of a worn tool = best affordable now, even if lower** (critique r2 #13, resolving the 2026-11-08 "never downgrade"
       against the 2026-11-09 "best tier the stockpile can afford at that moment"): the worker is **never downgraded while its tool
       works**, because M9 hands out the best tool first, so the worn iron axe stays in use until it breaks and only then is the new stone
       axe used. Ordering early costs nothing and saves the idle craft time. (DESIGN records it.)
     - **Pace-independent needs** (critique r2 #15): the farmer's hoe (×1.25 any tier) and the hunter's sword (the trip pace ignores the
       tier, M17) take the **cheapest sound tier**: STONE > WOODEN > COPPER > IRON (stone first: 131 uses for 2 cobblestone; iron only
       when nothing else is there). Guard swords and armour and the lumberjack's axe and miner's pickaxe take the best tier.
     - **Diamond rule** (diamonds are scarce; critique r2 #6): a DIAMOND item only when (a) it is a lumberjack axe, a miner pickaxe or
       guard gear; (b) **every other** demand-relevant key has an IRON-or-better item: `VillageGear.ironOrBetter: Map<OrderKey, Boolean>`
       over every tool slot (one per hired tool worker) and guard slot, the demanding key excluded, a not-loaded guard true, a key with an
       open iron-or-better order true (otherwise two demands block each other); gold is not iron-or-better, netherite is; (c) the diamonds
       left after the cost are ≥ `SMITH_DIAMOND_RESERVE` *(tunable, default 5; set it high to forbid diamonds)*. Else iron is tried.
     - **Leather** (armour only): only when iron and copper (ingots + smeltable raw ore) cannot cover the piece (r1's rule, kept).
     - **Ranks of tools the smith does not make** (critique r2 #12): `GearInfo.rank` for comparisons: wooden 1, leather 1, stone 2, gold
       3 (= copper), copper 3, chainmail 3, iron 4, diamond 5, netherite 6.
     - Nothing affordable → no order; `deficits` reports the cheapest missing material ("Needs planks" / "Needs iron") for the status.
       A demand with no affordable tier is **not orderable** and blocks nothing (row 12, critique r2 #16).
   - **Cancel** (re-checked before every unit and every `SMITH_CANCEL_CHECK_TICKS` 100 of a long craft): the order's key is no longer a
     demand slot **for a definite reason**: a not-worn tool of the kind now covers it (a player's tool), the hired count dropped (fired,
     dead), or the building is unregistered; and that on **two checks in a row** (hysteresis). `UNKNOWN` pauses. **r3**: this is M19a's
     `GearDemandPlanner.cancel(SlotPresence, consecutiveDefinite) → CancelCheck`, fed for tool slots by M19a's `ToolDemand.presence(camp,
     openOfCamp)` and for guard slots by M19's `GuardDemand.presence` (same `SlotPresence` values); the counter lives in `SmithRuntime`.
     Then: before commit the
     reservations are released and whatever the smith carries for the order (fetched inputs, sticks or ingots it already made; the fuel is
     spent) is deposited to Storage (critique r2 #18); after commit the committed materials are refunded (3.6, the golem refund rule) and the
     progress is lost (no tool is finished for nobody). A finished tool whose key vanished goes to Storage.
   - **Classification freeze** (critique r2 #10): once an order is committed its slot counts as open until the craft ends or a definite
     cancel reason appears; a guard piece becoming merely *repairable* is not one.
   - Pure API (r3: added to M19a's `GearDemandPlanner`): `next(List<GearDemand> demands, Map<CampNeed, List<OpenOrder>> openByCamp,
     Set<Urgency> classes, Materials m, int smeltable, VillageGear g, SmithSettings s) → Optional<GearOrder>` (a camp is orderable while
     its open orders < its demand slots; the order gets the camp's lowest unused slot index); M19a's `cancel(SlotPresence, int)` and
     `surplus(List<OpenOrder>, int needed)` unchanged; `deficits(List<GearDemand>, Materials) → List<Deficit>` (M19a's `Deficit(need,
     cheapestMaterial, short_)`, one per need) for the hint.
   - **Effect of the vanilla speeds (A9)**: an iron axe fells 1.5× as fast as stone and a wooden one half as fast, so the best-tier
     choice and the SMELT prerequisite matter a lot (one iron tool is worth half a stone-tooled worker more). That is why the planner takes
     the best affordable *now* and why WORN orders start a whole craft early: a worker without a tool loses all its output, a slow tier half.
5. **Gear repair** (core `RepairRules`): a unit restores `maxDamage / 4` (integer division) **[verified: `AnvilMenu` divides

   is written into DESIGN §5.7 as ingot-saving); the material is whatever `ItemStack.isValidRepairItem(material)` **[verified]** accepts among
   `iron_ingot`, `gold_ingot`, `copper_ingot`, `leather`, and `diamond` under the diamond reserve (r2, critique r2 #23: diamond gear is now
   made, and a repair is far cheaper than 8 new diamonds; tags `repairs_copper_armor` / `copper_tool_materials` = copper_ingot, `repairs_diamond_armor` /

6. **Pace and time.** Every clock is `WorkClock(ticksPerUnit).tick(WorkSpeed.untooled(level, needs))`. **Smelt batches and repairs**
   start only when they can end by `SMITH_DONE_BY` = `WORK_END − 500` (M13's rule: outputs reach Storage before GO_HOME). **Crafts**
   are too long for that (a diamond pickaxe is half a day), so a craft is **committed** like a golem build: at the anvil the inputs leave
   the smith's inventory into a saved `SmithCraft` (3.6) whose progress is saved every 200 ticks; it pauses outside WORK and continues
   the next day, by this or the next smith of that blast furnace. A craft may start at any WORK time. At GO_HOME the smith keeps nothing
   (keep 0), except items being returned to a Barracks chest and a finished tool on its way to a job chest, which it delivers first.

      every 40 ticks, through the door, stopping within 2.5), and within `GOLEM_REPAIR_REACH` 3.0 of the golem every `GOLEM_REPAIR_TICKS` 40: remove 1 ingot, `golem.heal(25)` **[verified: vanilla
      `IronGolem.mobInteract` heals 25 per ingot and plays `IRON_GOLEM_REPAIR`]**, the same sound, swing. It stops when `ingotsFor` is 0
      or it has no ingot left (then fetches more, row 4 again).
    - **How much** (`GolemRules.ingotsFor`): heal while the missing health is ≥ `GOLEM_REPAIR_MIN_MISSING` 10, 25 per ingot: missing
      m → `m < 10 ? 0 : ceil((m − 9) / 25)`. 40 HP → 3 ingots (40 → 65 → 90 → 100); 49 → 2 (99); 89 → 1; 91 → 0. Iron golems do not
      regenerate, so the numbers are exact.
11. **Vanilla golem spawning is off inside the claim** (mixin `VillagerGolemSpawnMixin`). Every villager-made golem goes through
    `Villager.spawnGolemIfNeeded(ServerLevel, long, int)` **[verified]**: it is called from `Villager.gossip` (5 villagers must agree;
    called by `TradeWithVillager`, the gossip at meetings) and from `VillagerPanicTrigger.tick` (3 villagers, the panic path, which the
    worker brain also has in CORE) **[verified bytecode: the only two callers]**. One `@Inject(method = "spawnGolemIfNeeded", at =
    @At("HEAD"), cancellable = true)`: cancel when a claim exists and the villager is within `CLAIM_RADIUS + 8` of the Heart (same
    dimension): vanilla places the golem up to 8 blocks from the villager (`trySpawnMob(..., 10, 8, 6, ...)` **[verified]**), so a
    villager just outside the edge could otherwise drop one inside (critique #21; documented). Count it only when vanilla would really
    have tried, i.e. when the public `wantsToSpawnGolem(gameTime)` **[verified]** is true for this villager (critique #2):
    `GolemGate.blocked()`, metric `golems.vanilla_blocked`; every call is also counted (`GolemGate.calls()`). Not touched: a player's iron-block-and-pumpkin golem

    event, and an `ENTITY_LOAD` filter cannot tell a summoned golem from one loaded from disk. Cost: one claim lookup per call (a villager

## 6. Risks & unknowns, out of scope

**Risks** (highest first):
1. **(top) Golem walking and waiting.** A 1.4 × 2.7 golem must path to a spot next to a room, stand there while vanilla goals and the
   targetSelector keep running, and not be pushed away by villagers. The vanilla goals were read (3.2 #10) but their interplay with a
   priority-1 MOVE goal is untested; `MoveBackToVillageGoal` and being shoved can still move it. Mitigations: the smith comes to the
   golem (re-targeted walk, reach 3.0) rather than the other way round, the wait spot is a checked 2×2×3 cell 3 out from the door, the
   waiting golem's navigation is stopped every tick, one golem per Smithy at a time, and T-L2-2/2b/2c/2d/2e check walk, wait, combat,
   queueing and reloads separately. If priority 1 misbehaves (for example `MoveBackToVillageGoal` still wins because the claim has no
   vanilla village POIs), stop the other movers while waiting with `goalSelector.disableControlFlag(Goal.Flag.MOVE)` (re-enabled in `stop()`) **[verified `GoalSelector.disableControlFlag/enableControlFlag`]**, and record it in §8.
2b. **The gate counts on `wantsToSpawnGolem` matching vanilla's own filter.** If T-L2-3's pre-round assertion fails (fewer than 5
   villagers want a golem) the test is wrong, not the gate: fix the setup, never relax the assertion.
2. **T-L2-1 runs ~9000+ ticks (and is bounded at 24 000)**: slow (several minutes) and exposed to M9-style walking flakes. The build
   itself is a fixed clock; only the fetch walk varies. Keep the Storage 4-6 blocks from the Smithy door.
3. **Three unmerged dependencies** (M13 pipeline, M18 guards/Barracks, M14/M15 numbers). Each has a fallback in 3.1; the build gate is
   M13 + M18 on dev. If M13's shape differs, step 8 adapts and records it rather than writing a second fetch system.
4. **Mixins** into `Villager.spawnGolemIfNeeded` and `IronGolem.registerGoals`: another mod doing the same could conflict; both are
   small HEAD/TAIL injects (`defaultRequire 1` makes a mismatch fail at start-up, not silently).
5. **Gear census blind spots**: unloaded guards count as fully equipped; golems in unloaded chunks are not counted (the cap can be
   exceeded by golems that wandered out of loaded chunks; golems stroll within the village, so this is rare). Both documented.
6. **Ingot competition**: worker and guard demands first, then a golem. A village with many guards and little iron gets no golem until
   the guards are equipped; that is the intended priority, but tuning (M26) may want a golem earlier.
7. **(r3: resolved by M19a) No tools before Town.** r2 flagged that the Town-tier Smithy cannot bootstrap a new village. The owner's
   answer (2026-11-09) is M19a: from Hamlet on, tool workers make their own wooden and stone tools, slowly; the Smithy unlocks the
   better tiers and takes over new demands. What remains for M19 is the **hand-off** at the first Town tier-up: self-crafts already
   running finish, no new ones start (except M19a's 3000-tick fallback), and the two sides must share one worn line and one order
   registry, or a self-craft is cancelled for nothing or a slot gets two tools. Mitigations: `GearOrders` is the only registry,
   `GearLead` is the only lead (3.2 #4), orders are counted per camp; T-L2-5l and T-L2-5m test it with the real service.
7b. **(r3) Changing M19a's code.** M19 appends to M19a's enums and adds methods to its classes (§7c). An append that changes an
   ordinal-based lookup breaks M19a silently (`CraftTier.selfTicks` indexes `SELF_TOOL_TICKS` by ordinal on `origin/m19a`; codecs store
   names). Mitigation: step 0 and steps 1-2 keep M19a's whole L1 and L2 suites green and unchanged; `CraftTier.rank()` replaces every
   ordinal comparison.
8. **(r2) Throughput.** One smith makes about 4 iron tools a day at level 1; a Town with 8+ tool workers and 2+ guards needs ~10 tools
   plus 2 × (sword + 4 armour) = 20 iron items just to start, ≈ 5 days of smithing. That is the intended slowness, but U1 workers may stand
   idle for days at the first Town tier-up if the player supplies nothing. Mitigation: U1 first, WORN orders start two units early,
   Storage tools are delivered without crafting, several blast furnaces = several smiths. M26 tunes the times.
9. **(r2) Demand census cost and blind spots.** The census reads every tool building's chests every 100 ticks (≤ ~10 buildings × 27 slots);
   an unloaded or unscanned camp gives no demand (never craft blind). A worker fired mid-craft cancels and refunds, which wastes the
   smith's time but never materials.
10. **(r2) Gold under A9.** With vanilla speeds (now merged) a gold tool is ×6.0 (3× stone) but lasts 32 uses. The smith never makes
   gold, but `ToolAllocation` ranks by `ToolTier` ordinal (GOLD between COPPER and IRON), so a player's golden axe is handed out after
   iron ones; the tool-speed change kept that ranking. M19 does not depend on it (the demand rule ignores tier). For the smith's worn
   line a gold tool's `unitTicksAt` is the gold speed (lumberjack 14 ticks per log), so its lead covers the fast wear.

**Out of scope**: a Heart UI for the diamond reserve or craft times (Balance only); spare stock of any kind (owner, 2026-11-09);
proactive upgrades of working tools; gold, chainmail and netherite crafting (netherite is upgrade-only with a smithing template);
shovels and spears (no job needs them); bows (string); repairing a worn tool **in a camp chest** instead of replacing it (a later
option: one ingot per quarter is cheaper than 3 for a new tool; v1 replaces); repairing items in job chests other than the Smithy and
Barracks; the tool-speed change itself (A9, merged separately); self-made tools (M19a); smelting ore blocks,
raw-ore blocks or other furnace recipes; enchanting; anvil `REPAIR_COST`; golems following orders, patrol routes or names; golems for
other villages; a player's own golems being refused (only villager-made spawns are gated).

## 8. Deviations

Checked against this branch (dev plus the M19 plan) before the core layer. M13, M14, M18, M19a and M22 are merged, so none of the "if not merged" fallbacks are used.

**Assumptions.** A1 holds: `FuelTable`, `Ingredient` and `BatchMath` stay in `core.cook` (the cook javadoc already says the smith shares them). The heat bank is still the field `smokerHeat` with codec key `smoker_heat`; step 5 will rename the field to `furnaceHeat` and keep that key. A2 holds: `Stockpiles.nearestHolding(level, from, key, holder)` and `withdrawAt(level, anchor, key, n, holder)`. A3 holds: `WorkSpeed.untooled`. A4 holds: guards, `GearItem`, `GearSlot` and the Barracks chest are on dev. A5 holds: `Population.count` and the Smithy is already `BuildingType` Town. A6 holds: `WorkUnit.ITEM_SMITHED` is already on the enum (not inserted). A7–A12 hold, including `GEAR_MIN_DURABILITY` 0.1 and the merged `TOOL_SPEED` table. M19a names match §2: `SmithService` is the `CURRENT` holder (C13), `GearLead.forCamp` returns 0 until a smith is registered, `PlankTable` is in `core.gear`.

**Enums.** `GearNeed` appends HEAD, CHEST, LEGS, FEET. `CraftTier` appends LEATHER, COPPER, IRON, DIAMOND. `Urgency` appends U2_GUARD_WEAPON and U3_GUARD_ARMOUR. `MaterialRole` appends the ingot, diamond, leather and raw copper/iron roles. No `RAW_GOLD`: gold smelting is addressed by the item id. Compare with `rank()`, never ordinal. `CraftTier.selfTicks()` throws for anything but wood and stone, so the self-craft codec cannot be asked for a copper duration. `GearNeed.toolKind()` throws for armour. `ToolRecipes.build` iterates only wood/stone and `GearNeed.tools()`, so growing the enums does not grow M19a's eight-row table. `ToolRecipes.ALL` is still those eight objects, and the wooden/stone rows of `SmithRecipes` are the same instances. Higher tiers and armour are `ToolRecipe` shapes that live only in `SmithRecipes`. Copper, iron and diamond tool heads use the vanilla tags (`#minecraft:copper_tool_materials` and the iron and diamond tags). Armour uses the item ids. The T-L1-1 words "copper_ingot, iron_ingot, diamond" are the single member of each tag.

**Tests that had to change because the enums grew.** `TierChoiceTest` loops `GearNeed.tools()` instead of `GearNeed.values()`, because `selfMade` refuses armour. `SelfCraftJobsGameTests` counts the four non-armour needs instead of asserting `values().length == 4`, and its grid switch has armour cases that throw. Self-craft behaviour is unchanged. These are not weakened assertions: the tool count is still four.

**Diamond reserve.** The rule sentence says diamonds left after the cost must be at least `SMITH_DIAMOND_RESERVE`. The pinned example (8 diamonds, a pickaxe costs 3, reserve 5, result iron) is the opposite of `>=`, because 5 is not greater than 5. The code uses a strict `>`. Mutation check: flipping it to `>=` makes `diamondsStayInReserveUnlessEveryoneElseIsAlreadyIron` expect an iron pickaxe and get a diamond one.

**Deficits.** A best-tier demand (axe, pickaxe, guard, armour) is priced at its last resort, so a lumberjack with only gold and sticks is short of **planks**, not cobblestone. Wood is a real order (five planks make a wooden axe; four are short one plank). A pace-independent job (hunter, farmer) is priced at stone, so the same stock is short of cobblestone. Gold is not a recipe and not iron-or-better. Netherite is iron-or-better. The census passes those as booleans; the planner never sees the item.

**Guard gear, same rule as tools.** `GuardDemand` asks for one MISSING slot per short piece, swords (U2) before armour (U3), in the order sword, chest, legs, head, feet. A piece counts only in WORN, BARRACKS, STORAGE or CARRIED. The Smithy chest does not cover a guard. A broken piece counts while a repair is already queued, and that coverage does not cancel a committed order (`presence` stays HELD). A real usable piece does cancel it (COVERED). `OneStack` gives a stored sword to a tool worker before a guard, one stack to one demand. The destination on the demand is Storage; the order key's building is the Barracks.

**Worn line.** `WearProfile.unitTicksAt` scales lumberjack and miner by `TOOL_SPEED[stone] / TOOL_SPEED[tier]`. Hunter and farmer stay on their stone-pace unit time. The smith's lead is `SmithRecipes.replacementLead`: the craft time of the nearest tier the smith makes (gold uses copper's time, netherite uses diamond's) plus `SMITH_DELIVERY_LEAD` 600, and 0 when no smith serves. `GearLead.forCamp` is still that 0 until step 10 registers `SmithService`. A worn slot's `currentRank` is `GearInfo.rank` (stone is 2, not the `ToolTier` ordinal).

**Planner.** `SmithFacts` is a mutable bag (`morning()` fills a valid idle morning) rather than a 30-field record. `SmithDecision.FetchAndDeliver` carries the order key and the item id. A short fetch is `readyToCommit` (false when anything in the plan's take is missing); there is no extra decision. `GOLEM_LAST_START` is inclusive: day time 9000 may still start a build, 9001 may not. The planner table says `<=`. A new smelt or repair must be able to finish by its deadline (the repair deadline is `SMITH_DONE_BY`, a fetch-for-repair's is that minus 1000), not merely start before it. A committed craft and a delivery are not cut off by `SMITH_DONE_BY`; both stop at `WORK_END`. `NEEDS_FUEL` is used only when ore without fuel is the only reason to be idle. A deficit wins, then "the golem cap is the only blocker", then fuel, then nothing to do. A `DELIVERY_BLOCKED` key is not fetched and not crafted this tick, and it still counts as orderable so it blocks a new golem. A waiting golem is the only decision that keeps the current batch (`keepsCurrent` / `currentAfter`). An orphaned furnace is chosen by `adoptFurnace` (a smith with no craft of its own, not its own furnace); the caller re-keys, and the planner continues whatever `savedCraft` it is then given. One owner ticks a saved golem build; the other skips that row. U1–U3 pre-empt a saved golem. U4 does not. A craft in progress is pre-empted only by the hurt golem.

**Golem cap.** `golems + builds < population / 5`. Mutation check: flipping `<` to `<=` makes `oneGolemPerFiveVillagers` allow a second golem at population 5.

**Saved data (step 5).** `golemBuilds` and `smithCrafts` are appended to `VillageState`. The codec stays one flat compound: `golem_builds` and `smith_crafts` are two more fields on `RAW_B` (9 fields, under the 16-field group limit). A golem progress above 8000 is clamped and kept (9000 loads as 8000); a negative progress is dropped by the lenient list, because a build has no `committed` list to refund from. A smith craft above its recipe ticks is clamped; a negative progress, an unknown recipe or an unknown need loads with `refund_pending` and its committed items intact. `self_crafts` is unchanged. The heat map's Java name is `furnaceHeat`. `smokerHeat()` still returns that map and the codec key is still `smoker_heat`, so M13 call sites and old saves do not move. There is no `furnace_heat` key.

**Spawn gate (step 6).** `VillagerGolemSpawnMixin` cancels `spawnGolemIfNeeded` at HEAD when `GolemGate.block` says the villager would really have tried (`wantsToSpawnGolem`) and stands within `CLAIM_RADIUS + 8` of the Heart, same dimension. Distance uses the villager's position against the Heart block. Every call increments `calls()`; only a real would-spawn inside the margin increments `blocked()`. A carved pumpkin still builds a golem (T-L2-9).
