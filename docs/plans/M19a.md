## 1. Goal

A hired lumberjack, miner, hunter or farmer that has no usable tool in its job chest (or whose tool is about to wear out) **makes its own
tool** from Storage materials: it walks to Storage, takes the materials, goes to its job block and works on the tool for a long time
(wooden 2400, stone 4800 ticks at level 1), during its own work time, with swings, sounds and particles and the status "Making a stone
pickaxe". It makes **stone** when the stockpile has cobblestone (or blackstone / cobbled deepslate) and sticks or planks, otherwise
**wooden** from planks, sticks or logs; never copper or better (that is the Smithy's, M19). A tool that works is never replaced, and a
tool the player puts in the chest is used at once and cancels the craft with a full refund. So a new village starts with wooden tools,
and as the miners bring cobblestone in, the next replacements are stone.

### 3.2 Decisions

1. **Who makes a tool: the worker that lacks it, for its own camp.** Demand is counted **per camp (building) and need**, as in M19 r2
   (tools sit in a chest the camp's workers share, `ToolAllocation` hands them out). Pure `ToolDemand.of(...)` (3.3):
   - `hired` = the camp's hired workers of that job in hiring order; `tools` = every tool of the kind in the camp containers (any tier,
     any origin, including gold, netherite, enchanted, bows/crossbows for the hunter). A tool is **worn** by the worn line (#3), else
     **fresh**.
   - `needed = max(0, hired − fresh)` demand slots `OrderKey(building, need, slot)`, slot 0 first. Slot `s` is **MISSING** when
     `hired − (fresh + worn) > s`, else **WORN** (M19's rule, unchanged).
   - Per worker, a full `ToolAllocation.assign` over all tools (worn included, the same ranking `CampTools.lease` uses) gives its own
     state: **FRESH** (holds a fresh tool), **WORN** (holds a worn one), **MISSING** (holds none).
   - A worker **may self-craft** when its own state is MISSING or WORN and the camp's open orders (`GearOrders`, #9) are fewer than
     `needed`; its order takes the lowest slot index no open order uses (the index is only an identifier). So the toolless worker (idle
     anyway) makes the missing tool, and the worker whose tool is wearing out makes its replacement; a worker with a fresh tool never
     crafts for a colleague (that would cost a working worker's output). (Workers holding a worn or no tool can only outnumber `needed`
     while the camp has an unassigned fresh tool, and then no worker is MISSING, so MISSING and WORN workers never compete for a slot.)
   - **Presence is counted per camp, not per slot index** (critique #3): an open order is **held** while its rank among the camp's open
     orders for that need (most progress first, ties by start tick) is below `needed`; otherwise it is surplus (`COVERED`). So when one of
     two orders finishes and `needed` drops from 2 to 1, the other one is still held, whatever its slot index.
   - **A crafting worker is left out of the tool allocation** (critique #4): `CampTools.lease` and the per-worker states skip workers with
     an open order (fetching or committed). They do not use a tool while crafting anyway, so the tool a worker finishes goes to a worker
     who is not crafting (normally itself), and a worn tool its crafter is not using meanwhile may serve a colleague. A worker holding a worn tool while
     `needed` is 0 (a fresh tool lies unused in the chest because its worn one ranks higher, e.g. a worn iron axe and a fresh stone one)
     crafts nothing: the fresh one takes over when the iron breaks.
   - The chest unreadable (no scan, unloaded, `CampContainers.of` empty) or the building registered but invalid → **UNKNOWN**: nothing new
     starts and an open craft **pauses** (never cancelled; M19 critique r2 #1).
2. **Need per job** (`GearNeed`, M19's enum): lumberjack AXE, miner PICKAXE, hunter SWORD (its `BOW_OR_SWORD` need; a bow needs string),
   farmer HOE. **Urgency** (M19's `Urgency`, used for ordering by M19's smith and for the status here): U1 MISSING of a REQUIRED tool,
   U4 WORN of a REQUIRED tool, U5 the farmer's hoe (MISSING or WORN). U2/U3 (guards) are M19's.
   - **The farmer** works without a hoe at ×1.0 and a hoe only gives ×1.25 (any tier, 1 wear per harvest). Making one costs 2400-4800
     ticks of work for ~59-131 harvests × 20 ticks × 20 % ≈ 240-520 ticks saved: a loss if it cost harvest time. So the farmer makes its hoe
     **only in idle time**: a farmer self-craft (start and every tick of progress) runs only while `FarmJob.hasWork(level, v)` is false.
     `hasWork` builds a **fresh** `FarmerPlanner` snapshot on every self-craft re-plan (every 20 ticks, also while the craft owns the
     tick; side-effect free: no status writes, no claims) and is true unless the decision's action is `IDLE_AT_FARMHOUSE` on a valid
     workplace, whatever its status change (critique #1, #2: `NO_WORK` is only given when there is no farmland at all, and a stored
     decision would go stale while the craft owns the tick). When crops ripen it goes back to farming and the craft pauses (progress
     kept). Farmers idle a lot (they wait for crops), so the hoe still comes. No "Needs hoe" status is ever shown (M10 rule kept).
3. **Worn line** (`ToolWear.worn`, shared with M19): `remaining < max(ceil(GEAR_MIN_DURABILITY × max), 2 × wearPerUnit, wearPerUnit ×
   ceil(leadTicks / unitTicks))`, `GEAR_MIN_DURABILITY` 0.1 *(tunable)*. `leadTicks` is how long the old tool must still last after the
   replacement is ordered: **0 for a self-craft** (the worker does not use its tool while it makes the next one, so nothing has to be
   bridged), `craftTicks + 600` for M19's smith. Self-craft lines (pinned in T-L1-3): stone axe 131 → worn below 14; wooden axe 59 →
   below 6; wooden pickaxe 59 → below 20 (two hauls); stone pickaxe 131 → below 20; iron pickaxe 250 → below 25; wooden sword 59 (hunter,
   5 per trip) → below 10; bow 384 → below 39; hoe 59 → below 6. A tool with `getMaxDamage() == 0` (not damageable) is never worn.
   **Why order early at all**: ordering at the line costs nothing (the worn tool is used up afterwards, #4) and the camp never stands
   without a tool when it breaks mid-tree; it is also the same line M19's smith uses, so the census is one function.
4. **Worn tools are used up first within a tier** (a change to `CampTools`'s tie order, so nothing is wasted): among tools of the same
   rank, the one with **less durability left** is handed out first (then container, then slot). So after a worker made its replacement it
   keeps using the worn one of the same tier until it breaks, then the new one; a better worn tool (iron) is still used before a fresh
   lower one (stone), as M9 already does by tier. Implemented as a `remaining` component on `ToolAllocation.Tool` (the old 3-argument
   constructor stays, remaining 0) and a comparator chain rank ↓, remaining ↑, order ↑ in `assign` (no packed int: a modded tool's
   durability could overflow it; critique #13), used by `CampTools.lease` for every job including the hunter's ranked lease. M15's side-by-side test already relies on this order (its worn axe is in slot 0); M9's lease
   test does not mix durabilities within a tier. L1 + L2 (T-L1-5, T-L2-12).
5. **Tier choice (self-made)**: **stone if affordable, else wooden, else nothing**; never copper, gold, iron, diamond or netherite, whatever
   the stockpile holds (`TierChoice.selfMade`). Stone is also preferred for hoes and hunters' swords (M19 r2's "cheapest sound tier":
   stone first). Why stone over wooden: under A1 a stone tool is 2× as fast, lasts 131 instead of 59 uses, and costs 4800 instead of
   2400 craft ticks; per log at level 1 in a dense forest that is ~(131 × 40 + 4800) / 131 ≈ 77 ticks against (59 × 80 + 2400) / 59 ≈
   121 (today's speeds: 77 against 91), so stone wins either way.
   - **Afford** = for every input, `available = stockpile count − reserved by others` (M20 builder reservations, other self-crafts),
     plus what this order already reserved. Reservations are per concrete `StackKey` (`core.stock.Reservations<StackKey>`), so `Materials`
     is built from **per-key** available counts (component-free keys only, each minus `reserved(key)`), grouped by role (planks, logs,
     stone material, stick) for the choice; `CraftPlan` then names the **concrete keys and counts** to reserve and withdraw (a mixed take
     such as 2 oak + 1 spruce planks is two keys; critique #7).
   - **Recipes** (vanilla counts **[verified data]**): axe and pickaxe 3 head + 2 sticks; hoe 2 + 2; sword 2 + 1; head material
     `#minecraft:stone_tool_materials` (= cobblestone, blackstone, cobbled_deepslate **[verified tag]**, mixed allowed as in vanilla
     crafting) for stone, `#minecraft:wooden_tool_materials` (= `#minecraft:planks` **[verified tag]**, mixed allowed) for wooden. Sticks
     `minecraft:stick` ← 2 `#minecraft:planks` → 4 (`minecraft:stick` recipe **[verified]**); planks ← 1 of `#minecraft:logs` → 4 of that
     wood's planks (all 11 `<wood>_planks` recipes take 1 `#<wood>_logs` / `#crimson_stems` / `#warped_stems` and give 4 **[verified]**;
     bamboo blocks give 2 and are not used).
   - **Material order**: sticks from Storage if it has enough for the recipe, else one STICKS step (2 planks → 4 sticks; stockpile sticks
     are then not touched, never a mix); planks from Storage before logs; logs only for the planks still missing, `ceil(missing / 4)` of
     them. A wooden tool needs planks for head **and** sticks: wooden axe from scratch = 3 + 2 planks = 2 logs. Example: 2 oak logs →
     wooden axe, leftovers 3 oak planks + 2 sticks.
   - Only component-free input stacks are taken (`StackKey.patch().isEmpty()`; the M13/M19 rule: no renamed planks).
6. **Usable tool in Storage first.** Before crafting, if the stockpile holds a tool of the need's kind that is not worn, carries no
   enchantment and no custom name (the player's special items stay where they are), the worker fetches it into its job chest instead of
   crafting (`FETCH_STORED`; no craft; M19 r2's `FetchAndDeliver` rule, which M19 should loosen from "component-free" to this predicate so
   a merely damaged tool counts too; noted in 3.8).
7. **Where and how slowly.**
   - **Fetch**: the worker reserves the inputs (`reservations.set(uuid, key, n)`), walks to the nearest Storage holding the head material
     (`Stockpiles.nearestHolding`, `WalkTo`, reach as `BuilderJob.fetch`), and there **commits**: it withdraws exactly the planned raw
     inputs (`Stockpiles.withdraw(..., holder = uuid)`) straight into a saved `SelfCraft` record (3.6), never into its inventory. So a
     deposit trip, a death or the miner's "hand in everything" rule can never touch them, and a cancel refunds exactly what was taken. If
     a withdraw comes back short (the player took items meanwhile), everything already withdrawn goes back (`Stockpiles.deposit`), the
     reservations are released and the worker re-plans at once (never a partial commit).
   - **Craft**: then it walks to its **job block** (`WorkerData.jobBlock`: chopping block, stonecutter, fletching table, composter),
     stands within `Balance.JOB_BLOCK_REACH` (1.5) of it, looks at it, and runs `WorkClock(SELF_TOOL_TICKS[tier]).tick(WorkSpeed.untooled(level, needs))`
     (level 2 ×1.25, level 3 ×1.5; Hamlet caps level 1). No crafting table is needed or used (a Lumber Camp may be outdoors-ish and
     every tool job already has its job block; a crafting-table station is out of scope).
   - **Times** *(tunable)*: `SELF_TOOL_TICKS` wooden **2400**, stone **4800** at level 1, 4× M19's smith (wooden 600, stone 1200). That
     is about a quarter / half of the ~10 000-tick work day.
   - **Visuals** (no held item: a villager renders its main hand, **[verified]** `VillagerRenderer.extractRenderState` →
     `HoldingEntityRenderState.extractHoldingEntityRenderState`, but a hired villager's main hand is the trade display, and `Mob` drops
     hand items on death with a chance: a fake held item could be duplicated or clash with trading). Every `SELF_CRAFT_HIT_TICKS` 100
     ticks of progress: `swing(InteractionHand.MAIN_HAND)` **[verified]**, `level.playSound(null, jobBlock, SoundEvents.WOOD_HIT /
     STONE_HIT, SoundSource.NEUTRAL, 0.6f, 1.0f)` **[verified fields and signature]**, 4 `ParticleTypes.ITEM` particles of the head material
     (`new ItemParticleOption(ParticleTypes.ITEM, stack)` **[verified constructor]**) on top of the job block via `ServerLevel.sendParticles`
     **[verified]**. On finish: `SoundEvents.VILLAGER_WORK_TOOLSMITH` **[verified]** and `HAPPY_VILLAGER` particles.
   - **Finish**: the tool (`new ItemStack(item)`, no components) goes into the camp's first container with an empty slot
     (`CampContainers.of`; checked before the fetch and again here). If the chest has filled up meanwhile, the tool goes to Storage
     (`Stockpiles.deposit`; with no room, the worker's inventory), status `TOOL_NO_SPACE`; the stored-tool fetch (#6) brings it once a
     slot is free (critique #11). The conversion leftovers (sticks, planks) go to Storage the same way. Then **worn tools nobody is
     assigned** (a full allocation over the camp gives them to no worker, e.g. a worn wooden pickaxe beside a fresh stone one) are moved
     from the camp chest to Storage, so camps do not fill up with them (critique #16); the stored-tool fetch never takes worn tools, so they
     wait there for the player. The record is removed in the same tick by **compare-and-remove** (only if the stored record still has this
     worker, key and recipe). Counters (`WorkStats.add(job, …)`): `tools_made.<tier>`, `tools_fetched`, `tools_cancelled`,
     `tools_to_storage`. No XP (it is not the job's work unit).
   - **Record writes** (critique #6): progress writes **only update** an existing record matched on worker + key (never insert one);
     only the commit inserts. Finish and refund remove by compare-and-remove. So a `stop()` after `SelfCraftSystem` refunded a fired worker
     cannot bring the record (and its committed items) back.
   - **Pause** outside WORK (the behaviour stops at 11 000; the record keeps the progress, saved every `SELF_CRAFT_SAVE_TICKS` 200 ticks
     and on `stop()`; a restart loses at most 200 ticks) and continue at the next WORK; also paused while the camp is UNKNOWN, while the
     worker is too hungry (`needs.speed() == 0`, through `untooled`) and, for the farmer, while it has farm work (#2).
8. **Cancel, refund, no proactive upgrade.**
   - A slot that no longer exists **for a definite reason** cancels: a fresh tool now covers it (`COVERED`, e.g. the player's tool),
     the worker is no longer hired at that building (`HIRED_DROPPED`: fired, dead, converted), or the building is unregistered
     (`BUILDING_UNREGISTERED`). Checked by `SelfCraftSystem` every `SELF_CANCEL_CHECK_TICKS` 100 ticks for **every** record, also while
     the craft is paused (overnight, the farmer busy, the worker unloaded), and only on **two definite checks in a row** (hysteresis;
     M19's rule). An UNKNOWN check neither counts nor resets the counter; a HELD check resets it. When a camp's open orders exceed
     `needed`, the one with the **least progress** is the surplus one (M19's `surplus`; presence per camp, #1). A building that stays
     registered but **invalid** for `SELF_CRAFT_INVALID_REFUND_DAYS` 3 work days refunds its records (materials are not locked for ever;
     an unloaded camp never does). **Before commit** (the fetch walk) there is no hysteresis: a slot gone at any re-plan, or in the census
     taken right before the commit, aborts the fetch (nothing has moved).
   - **Refund**: before commit, the reservations are released (nothing moved). After commit, the committed items go back to Storage
     (`Stockpiles.deposit`), the rest into the worker's inventory if it is loaded, else dropped at its building marker (only while that
     chunk is loaded; otherwise the refund waits, the record stays with `refundPending`). The progress is lost. The record is removed in
     the tick of the refund by compare-and-remove (no double refund).
   - **Demand only**: no slot, no craft. A worker with a fresh tool never crafts, whatever the stockpile holds (30 iron, 64 cobblestone):
     quality rises only through wear (owner, 2026-11-09, the same rule as M19). A worn tool's replacement is "stone if affordable, else
     wooden", even when the worn tool is iron: the iron one is still used first (#4), so the worker is never downgraded while it works.
9. **One order per slot.** `GearOrders` (transient glue, per server; M19 renames its planned `SmithOrders` to this, 3.8) maps open
   `OrderKey`s to the UUID of whoever serves them (a self-crafter here, a smith in M19), from reservation to finish. Rebuilt on server
   start and after a claim change from the saved `SelfCraft` records; a pre-commit order is lost on restart (its reservations are
   transient too).
10. **When a worker stops its job for a craft** (the boundary): a craft starts (and a paused one resumes) only between the job's units,
    so no tree, haul or trip is cut in half:
    - lumberjack: `LumberJob`'s **`ws.action`** (the running action, which turns `CONTINUE_FELL` as soon as a task starts; not
      `ws.decision`) is not `CONTINUE_FELL` or `COLLECT` (a MISSING lumberjack cannot fell anyway; it collects drops first, they despawn
      after 6000 ticks; `REPLANT` may wait). While the craft owns the tick, `guard()` and `backgroundSearch` do not run (intended: the
      search only matters for felling and resumes after the craft);
    - miner and hunter: on the surface/at the Lodge (`!AwayTrips.isAway`), nothing to hand in (`!MinerKeep.carriesNonFood`,
      `!HuntGear.carriesYield`), no pending haul;
    - farmer: idle (#2).
    A worn-tool craft therefore starts after the haul or tree that crossed the line. The craft stops the job: while it runs, the job's
    own tick returns early (one line, like `Workers.tripRunning`), so it costs the worker that much output: the trade-off of
    self-sufficiency.
11. **Status words** (Villagers tab, `WorkerStatus` + `ActivityKind`, appended): `MAKING_WOODEN_TOOL` / `MAKING_STONE_TOOL` while
    fetching and crafting (set from the runtime plan when the fetch starts, so the words are right before the commit; critique #8), shown as
    "Making a wooden axe" / "Making a stone pickaxe" / "Making a stone sword" (hunter) / "Making a wooden hoe"; a MISSING REQUIRED worker
    that cannot start shows "Needs axe (no wood in Storage)" (`TOOL_NO_MATERIALS`; "no wood" = no planks, sticks or logs to make the
    cheapest tier) or "Needs axe (camp chest full)" (`TOOL_NO_SPACE`); otherwise M9's "Needs axe" (e.g. while a smith will serve it, M19).
    The farmer shows nothing. The job's own planner keeps writing `NEEDS_TOOL` when the self-craft hook returns false, so the block reason
    lives in `SelfCraftRuntime` (cleared by the next re-plan that finds no block) and `HeartFacts` **prefers it over `NEEDS_TOOL`**
    (critique #9). No new snapshot field; the tool kind comes from the job, as `activity.needs_tool.<kind>` does today.
12. **Before Village tier** only lumberjacks and farmers exist (Hamlet), and a Hamlet's only stone comes from the player (miners are a
    Village unlock), so a Hamlet makes wooden tools unless the player stores cobblestone. Self-crafting works at every tier, also at
    Town before a smith is hired.

### 3.7 How fast a village goes from wood to stone (the owner's clarification)

Numbers at level 1, under A1 (tool-speed follow-up) and, in brackets, today's `Balance`:

- **A new village** with only wood (the player's logs or planks in Storage, or the lumberjack's first logs): every tool worker's first
  tool is wooden, made in 2400 ticks. 2 logs bootstrap one axe or pickaxe.
- **Lumberjack**: a wooden axe (59) fells at 80 ticks per log (today 50) when trees are dense, so it reaches its worn line (< 6) after
  ~54 logs, about **4300 felling ticks ≈ half a work day** (today ~2700); real camps walk more, so about one work day. Its replacement is
  the first chance to switch to stone.
- **Miner** (Village tier): with a wooden pickaxe a haul takes 4000 ticks (today 2500) and brings 16 cobblestone (Y ≥ 40) or 12
  cobblestone / cobbled deepslate (deeper; both are stone tool materials). So **its first haul, ~6500 ticks after hiring (craft + haul),
  puts enough stone in Storage for five stone tools**. Its wooden pickaxe is worn after 4 hauls (59 → 19 < 20), about 1.6 work days in;
  its replacement is stone.
- So in a village with a miner, **every replacement made from the second day on is stone**, and the wooden tools are all replaced
  within about **1-2 work days** of the first haul (lumberjack ~1 day, miner ~2 days). The hunter's wooden sword lasts 10 trips (5 work
  days at 2 trips a day; the sword's tier does not change the hunter's pace, M17) and the farmer's hoe is idle-time work, so those two
  switch later, which costs nothing.
- **Stone tools last**: stone axe 131 logs (worn below 14) ≈ 4700 felling ticks at 40 per log; stone pickaxe worn after 12 hauls (131 → 11 < 20), broken on the 14th, ≈ 2.8 work days of hauls.
  Each replacement costs the worker 4800 ticks (~half a day). In a dense forest a lumberjack spends ~45 % of its time on axes; that is the
  intended price of self-sufficiency (risk 2), which the player's tools and M19's smith (1200 ticks for a stone axe) remove.

`OpenOrder`, `MaterialRole`, `StockItem`, `CraftPlan`, `Deficit`, `TierChoice.selfMade`, `CancelReason`, `SlotPresence`, `CancelCheck`,
`GearDemandPlanner.cancel/surplus`,
`GearCraft`, `SupplyRule`; self-craft-only: `SelfCraft`, `PendingFetch`, `SelfCraftFacts`, `SelfCraftDecision`, `SelfCraftPlanner`. Glue
(`worker.gear`): `ToolStacks`, `PlankTable`, `ToolCensus`, `MaterialCensus`, `GearOrders`, `SmithService`, `SelfCraftJob`, `SelfCrafts`,
`SelfCraftSystem`, `SelfCraftSwitch`; plus the crafting-set skip in `CampTools.lease` and the `remaining` order in `ToolAllocation`.

**M19 then** (its plan needs an r3 note, flagged in PROGRESS): moves nothing; uses `core.gear` instead of defining these in `core.smith`;
appends `GearNeed` HEAD/CHEST/LEGS/FEET, `CraftTier` LEATHER/COPPER/IRON/DIAMOND, `Urgency` U2/U3, `GearInfo`; adds `TierChoice.best`,
`GearDemandPlanner.next/deficits`, `SmithRecipes` (whose wooden/stone tool rows are `ToolRecipes.ALL`, with the smith's own times),
`Materials` keys for ingots/raw ore; calls `ToolWear.worn(..., leadTicks = craftTicks + 600)` (M19 r2's formula, now with the 600
explicit); builds `DemandCensus` on `ToolCensus` + guards; uses `GearOrders` instead of `SmithOrders`; stores `SmithCraft(furnace,
GearCraft, destination)`; registers a `SmithService`.

**Hand-off rule** (`SupplyRule.selfCraftMayStart`, pure; M19 only supplies `smithServes`):
1. `smithServes` = **the village has at least one hired blacksmith at a registered, valid Smithy** (M19's `SmithService`; in M19a no
   service is registered, so it is always false; a hired-but-idle M7 smith on `dev` does not stop self-crafting).
2. While `smithServes`, **no new self-craft starts**, with one fallback: a MISSING slot of a REQUIRED tool that has had **no open order
   for `SELF_CRAFT_FALLBACK_TICKS` 3000 work ticks** (the smith is busy with a long craft, or cannot reach it) may be self-crafted, so a
   miner never stands idle for days because a smith exists. `slotOpenWorkTicks` counts WORK-time ticks since the slot appeared without
   an order (transient; reset by any order).
3. **An open order is never taken over**: a self-craft that has started (reserved or committed) finishes, whatever happens to the
   Smithy; the smith never takes a key that `GearOrders` holds, and a self-crafter never takes one the smith holds.
4. The worn line follows the server: with `smithServes` the census uses the smith's lead time (more tools count as WORN, the smith starts
   early); without, lead 0.
5. If the smith goes away (fired, Smithy invalid), `smithServes` turns false and self-crafting resumes at once.

## 6. Risks & unknowns, out of scope

**Risks** (highest first):
1. **(top) Older L2 tests change behaviour.** Any tool worker with no or a worn tool in a world whose Storage holds wood (lumberjacks
   deposit their logs) now stops to craft. The job tests were written when that could not happen; step 12 audits the whole suite with a
   strict rule (setup or the gametest-only switch, never an assertion). A missed one shows as a flake-like timing failure (a worker idle
   for 2400-4800 ticks), so the builder must read every newly red or slower test's log for `[m19a]` lines before calling it a flake.
2. **Self-sufficiency is expensive** by design: a stone tool costs half a work day; in a dense forest a lumberjack spends ~45 % of its
   time on axes (3.7). If that feels bad in play, M26 tunes `SELF_TOOL_TICKS` (it is one Balance array) or the worn line; the player's
   tools and M19's smith remove the cost. The owner asked for "slowly", so the plan keeps the proposed numbers.
3. **Interrupting four different job loops.** The hook is one line per job plus a boundary reader per job; a wrong boundary cuts a tree
   or a haul in half (M9's tree tasks are saved, so a cut tree is finished later; a miner hook while away cannot happen because the
   hidden miner does not tick). T-L2-1/5/6/9/14/15 cover each job.
4. **Farmer idle detection.** "Idle" is the planner's `IDLE_AT_FARMHOUSE`+`NO_WORK`; if the farmer flips between idle and a small task
   (bone meal, compost) every few ticks the craft barely moves. Acceptable (the hoe is a bonus); T-L2-15 checks it finishes.
5. **The seam is untested against the real smith.** M19 must keep the rule of 3.8 (its plan gets an r3 note). T-L2-16 tests the seam
   with a fake service.
6. **Wooden pickaxes mine iron** (BACKLOG M16 item: the wooden pickaxe uses the stone table, slower): self-made wooden pickaxes make this
   common. Not changed here (M16 rule as built); flagged again in BACKLOG.

**Out of scope**: copper or better tools (M19's smith, Town); shovels and spears (no job needs them); bows (string); a crafting-table
station or held-item animation; repairing tools; tools for unhired jobs or spare stock; a Heart UI for craft times; XP for crafting;
punching trees without an axe (a lumberjack with no wood anywhere waits for the player); the tool-speed follow-up itself (A1); the
optional L3 run (5.4).
