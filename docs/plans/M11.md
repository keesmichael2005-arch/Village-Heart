## 1. Goal

Every evening at tick 11000 the whole village eats from the stockpile: each adult needs 10 nutrition and each child 5, the best food
goes first and cooked food before raw. A villager who missed a dinner works at half speed the next day and stops working after two
missed dinners, but never dies of hunger. Workers sleep in House beds when they can; a worker without a bed in a valid House works at
×0.75. The `starter` simulation proves the loop in a real village: two Houses, Storage, a Farmhouse with a carrot field, a Lumber Camp
and a Kitchen, one farmer and one lumberjack, 10 days, nobody starves, ≥ 90 % of villager-days fed from day 3, and logs grow every day.

### 3.2 Decisions

1. **How villagers are fed: an abstract dinner, no walking.** At dinner the village system takes the food straight out of the Storage
   chests (`Stockpiles.withdraw`) for every villager in the claim, wherever they stand. Justification: DESIGN §6 defines dinner as
   "the village eats from the stockpile at 11000"; it does not ask anyone to fetch food. Walking 10-20 villagers to Storage would add
   pathfinding failures (FOLLOW_RANGE 16, unreachable doors, unloaded paths), would collide with M7's GO_HOME deposit trips in the same
   1000 ticks, and would change no outcome a player can see except the walk itself. It is also deterministic, so dinner is
   unit-testable end to end. Eating "at home" is cosmetic and out of scope (6). The stockpile is still the only food source:
   food carried in villager inventories (vanilla villagers keep bread and crops for breeding) is never eaten by dinner.
2. **Who eats:** every living, loaded `Villager` within the claim sphere (`ClaimGeometry.inClaim`, M7) of the Heart: workers,
   unemployed and vanilla-profession villagers, nitwits and children. Villagers in unloaded chunks are skipped and keep their state
   (no catch-up, DESIGN §6). The census moves from `SimMetricsCollector` into main code (`needs.VillageCensus`) so dinner and the metrics
   count the same villagers.
3. **When:** `core.food.DinnerClock`. Day index `d = floorDiv(dayTime, 24000)`, phase `floorMod(dayTime, 24000)`. Dinner for day `d`
   is due when `phase >= Balance.DINNER_TICK` (11000) and `d != lastDinnerDay` and the Heart's block is loaded (`level.isLoaded(heart)`).
   `!=`, not `>`: `/time set 1000` jumps the day index back to 0, and dinner must still fire that evening. A jump over the whole
   window (`/time add`, sleeping cannot skip it: beds work from 12542) skips that dinner: no catch-up, no hunger counted.
   A player sleeping through a thunderstorm before 11000 also skips that dinner (beds work at any time in a storm): documented, no catch-up.
   `lastDinnerDay` is saved in `VillageSavedData` (codec key `last_dinner_day`, optional). When it is absent (a new claim, an old save),
   the first tick sets it to `d` if `phase >= 11000`, else `d - 1`, so claiming at dusk does not serve an instant dinner.
   **The sim harness** sets the day time to 0 after its setup actions; right after that it calls `Dinner.resetClock(server)`
   (`last_dinner_day = -1`), so whatever the system did during setup, dinner of day index 0 fires. Sim day N (1-based) = day index N - 1.
4. **What each villager eats** (`core.food.DinnerPlanner`, pure):
   - Need: adult `ADULT_NUTRITION = 10`, child (`isBaby()`) `CHILD_NUTRITION = 5`.
   - **Food ranking** (`FoodRules.BEST_FIRST`): prepared before raw, then nutrition descending, then saturation descending, then
     item id ascending (deterministic). *Raw* = an item the Cook (M13) turns into something better: `beef, porkchop, chicken, mutton,
     rabbit, cod, salmon, potato` (namespaced `minecraft:`). Every other item with a `FOOD` component is *prepared* (bread, carrot,
     apple, cooked meat, stews, berries, …). Never eaten (`Balance.FOOD_NEVER_EAT`): `rotten_flesh, spider_eye, poisonous_potato,
     pufferfish, suspicious_stew, chorus_fruit, golden_apple, enchanted_golden_apple, golden_carrot` (harmful, teleporting, or a player's
     valuables). Items with nutrition ≤ 0 are ignored. Effects are never applied (villagers have no hunger bar).
   - **Order of eaters:** hungry days descending (the hungriest eat first, so short food spreads to those about to stop working), then
     workers in hiring order, then non-worker adults before children, then UUID. (Adults first so a child never takes the 8-point beef
     an adult needed; children are still fed from what is left, since they need only 5.)
   - **Greedy meal:** for each eater in order, if the food still available sums to at least its need, it eats the best item, then the
     next best, until it has eaten ≥ its need (a second 8-nutrition beef overshoots to 16: that is "best first" taken literally; a
     top-up optimiser is a balance question for M26). Otherwise it eats **nothing** (a partial meal would not make it fed, so it would
     only waste food) and later eaters with a smaller need (children) can still be fed.
   - **Fed** = ate ≥ need. The result: per eater `Meal(fed, nutrition, items)`, per key the amount consumed, and totals
     (`diners, fed, hungry, eatenNutrition, eatenItems`).
   - Available stock per key = snapshot count − `Reservations.reserved(key)` (M20's build reservations are never eaten).
5. **Hunger state** (`core.needs.Diet`, persistent attachment `villageheart:diet` on **every** villager, workers or not):
   `Diet(int epoch, int hungryDays, long lastDinnerDay, int lastNutrition)`. After a dinner: fed → `hungryDays = 0`, hungry →
   `hungryDays + 1`. A villager with no attachment, or whose `epoch` differs from the claim's epoch (the Heart was broken and placed again),
   reads as `Diet.FRESH` (fed, 0 days): new babies and newcomers start fed. Fabric attachments without `copyOnDeath` are not copied on
   zombie conversion (M7 3.4), so a cured villager starts fresh.
6. **Needs and work speed** (`core.worker.Needs`, M7's record): M11 adds the invariant `fed == (hungryDays == 0)` (compact constructor;
   the codec decodes a violating record **leniently**, `fed := hungryDays == 0`, with one WARN log, because a `DataResult.error` would
   make Fabric drop the whole `WorkerData` attachment and silently fire the worker) and fills `speed()`:
   `fedFactor(hungryDays) × (housed ? 1 : UNHOUSED_SPEED) × (paid ? 1 : 0)`, with `HUNGER_SPEED = {1.0, 0.5, 0.0}` indexed by
   `min(hungryDays, 2)` and `UNHOUSED_SPEED = 0.75` (all `Balance`, tunable). The effect starts at the dinner that set it, so it applies
   to the next work day. `WorkerData.needs` becomes a **derived copy**, written by one writer (`NeedsSystem`, 3.5): `Needs(diet.fed,
   diet.hungryDays, housed, paid)`. M9/M10 read it unchanged through `WorkSpeed`.
7. **Paid stub until M12:** `needs.PaidSource` (a `Predicate<WorkerRecord>`) with `ALWAYS = r -> true`. `NeedsSystem` writes
   `paid = PaidSource.current().test(record)`. M12 swaps the source and adds strikes; `Needs.speed()` already returns 0 for
   `paid = false` and is unit-tested now.
8. **Housed** (`needs.Housing`, glue over a pure `core.needs.HouseBeds`): a villager is housed when its brain's `HOME` memory
   (`GlobalPos`) is in the Heart's dimension and its position is a `#bed` head cell of the latest scan of (a) any **valid House**, or (b)
   for a worker, its own building (Barracks beds, M18). Vanilla bed POIs are on the bed **head** (`PoiTypes` registers only
   `BedPart.HEAD` states **[verified]**), matching the scan key. Buildings with no cached scan contribute no beds (contents unknown).
   The bed index is rebuilt every `NEEDS_REFRESH_TICKS = 100` from `Buildings.valid(server, HOUSE)` (a few sets of packed longs).
9. **House beds are preferred, for workers** (DESIGN §3.3 "each bed = 1 home"; M7 left this to M11): every refresh, for each loaded
   worker that is not housed and not sleeping, `NeedsSystem.rehome` picks the nearest House bed whose POI exists and has a free ticket
   (`poi.getInSquare(h -> h.is(PoiTypes.HOME), pos, 0, Occupancy.HAS_SPACE)` contains it **[verified signatures: `getInSquare`,
   `take`, `release`, `exists`, `getType`]**). If none is free, it may take one held by a **non-worker who is not sleeping**: that
   villager's `HOME` memory is erased and its ticket released (it finds another bed with its own vanilla brain). Then: release the
   worker's old HOME ticket if its POI exists, `poi.take(h -> h.is(HOME), (h, p) -> p.equals(bed), bed, 1)`, and set
   `HOME = GlobalPos.of(dim, bed)`. Workers never take each other's House beds. Unemployed villagers keep vanilla bed claiming.
   "Cannot have children" for the unhoused is M23's (it reads `Housing.housed`).
10. **Food days left** (`core.food.FoodMath.daysLeft(nutrition, adults, children)`) = stockpile nutrition ÷ (10·adults + 5·children),
    the real daily need. DESIGN §6 writes "population × 10"; this is the same rule with §6's own "children need half" applied, and is
    recorded in DESIGN as built. Empty population → `OptionalDouble.empty()` (the metric writes NaN → JSON `null`).
    Stockpile nutrition counts every edible, not-excluded item (raw included) at its nutrition × available count.
11. **Use remainders:** eating an item whose `USE_REMAINDER` is set (stews → bowl, honey bottle → glass bottle) deposits the remainder
    into the stockpile (`Stockpiles.deposit` from the Heart); what does not fit is popped at the Heart (`Block.popResource`), never lost.
12. **Existing tests keep their pace.** From step 3 on, every worker in an M7/M9/M10 GameTest would be unhoused (x0.75) and, past
    11000 without Storage, hungry. So `NeedsTestHooks` (gametest source set) has `forceDefaultNeeds` and `dinnerEnabled`;
    `VillageTestSupport.reset` sets `forceDefaultNeeds = true, dinnerEnabled = false` for every test, and only the M11 needs tests
    (environment prefix `needs_`) switch them back. The override makes `NeedsSystem` write `Needs.DEFAULT` and makes `DinnerSystem` skip.
    Main code reads it through one static (`NeedsOverride.current()`, null in production). L3 has no hooks: `bare` now gets dinners
    (no Storage, so everyone is "hungry"; nobody works there anyway).
13. **Statuses:** `WorkerStatus.TOO_HUNGRY` (hungryDays ≥ 2: "Too hungry to work"), lang `status.villageheart.too_hungry`. Precedence in
    `Workers.status`: `WORKPLACE_INVALID` > `TOO_HUNGRY` > job statuses (`NEEDS_TOOL`, …) > storage statuses > `OK`. A ×0.5 or ×0.75
    worker is still `OK` (the needs icons say why it is slow).
14. **Dinner happens once even with 2 dimensions or a reload**: the system reads only the Heart's level; `last_dinner_day` is saved
    with the village and written in the same tick as the meals.

### 3.4 Glue: dinner (`dev.villageheart.food`)

- `FoodCatalog.of(StackKey) : Optional<FoodValue>`: `key.toStack(1).get(DataComponents.FOOD)` (so components count), raw/never-eat from
  `FoodRules` by `key.id()`; cached per `Item` when the patch is empty.
- `Dinner.serve(ServerLevel level, BlockPos heart, long day) : DinnerResult`: census → eaters (`Diet.read`, `isBaby`, worker index from

  consumed key in ≤ 1-stack calls (`Stockpiles.withdraw(level, heart, key, n, null)`); if a withdraw returns less than planned
  (impossible in one tick, but cheap to guard) the shortfall is taken from that key's last eaters, who become hungry; remainders (3.2 #11);
  write each eater's `Diet.afterDinner`; set `last_dinner_day`; record `FoodStats` (3.8). One INFO log line per dinner:
  `[dinner] day D: F/N fed, X nutrition from Y items, Z food days left`.
- `DinnerSystem` (`VillageSystems.register("villageheart:dinner", …)`): every tick, inside its section, one `getDayTime()` read and

### 3.5 Glue: needs (`dev.villageheart.needs`)

- `VillageCensus.villagers(server) : List<Villager>` (moved from `SimMetricsCollector.census`, which now calls it).
- `Housing`: the `HouseBeds` index (rebuilt every refresh), `housed(villager)`, `houseBedCount()`.
- `NeedsSystem` (`villageheart:needs`, every `NEEDS_REFRESH_TICKS`, timed section): rebuild the index; for each loaded worker
  (`VillageCensus` ∩ attachment) compute `Needs(diet.fed, diet.hungryDays, housed, paid)` and write it only if it changed; `rehome`

- `Workers.hire` (M7) sets the new worker's needs from its diet and housing at once (a hungry villager stays hungry when hired);
  `fire` leaves the diet attachment alone (hunger survives a firing).
- Heart release: nothing to undo (diets carry the old epoch and read as fresh after a new claim).

### 3.6 Wiring into M9/M10 work speed

- `WorkSpeed.multiplier` already multiplies by `needs.speed()` (A2): M11 only fills `speed()`. So hungry ×0.5 halves the lumberjack's
  log pace (40 → 80 ticks per log with a stone axe) and the farmer's `WorkClock` units; unhoused ×0.75 → 53.3 ticks per log.
- Speed 0 must not mean "stand at the tree forever with a frozen crack": add a top row to `LumberjackPlanner` and M10's farmer planner:
  `needs.speed() == 0` → `IDLE_AT_CAMP` and status `TOO_HUNGRY` (or M12's strike). Deposit trips still run (a starving worker can still
  carry what it holds to Storage); felling, harvesting, planting, collecting and fetching do not. An interrupted fell keeps its saved
  task (M9 3.6) and resumes after the worker has eaten.
- Any later job (M13-M19) reads `WorkSpeed` and gets this for free; the planner row is part of the job toolkit pattern.

### 3.9 Harness work for `starter` (M2 extension)

Prebuilt structures are **code-built prefabs**, not structure templates. Templates would need hand-authored `.nbt` files, a marker
facing per rotation and a terrain fit we cannot see without running the game; a few dozen lines of block placing reuse the shapes the
GameTests already build (`BuildingTestSupport.room`), stay readable in the scenario JSON, and are GameTested in a yard (T-L2-12) so a
broken prefab is found in `verify.sh`, not 10 minutes into a simulation.

New action types (one `SimAction` each in `sim.SimActions.ALL`, ids in `ScenarioParser.ACTION_TYPES`; field shapes checked at parse time
by a pure `core.sim.ActionSpecs` so a typo fails in L1 `ScenarioFilesTest`, not at boot):

| Action | Fields | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `site` | `size [w, d]`, `min_distance`, `max_distance` (horizontal, from the Heart to the site's nearest / farthest corner) | Finds a flat, untouched area for the whole compound (pure `core.sim.SiteFinder` over a sampled height/cover grid): every column's top block is natural ground (grass, dirt, coarse dirt, podzol, sand, gravel) with only replaceable plants above, no water, logs, leaves, farmland, paths or village blocks; max height deviation from the median ≤ 3; all four corners `isPositionEntityTicking` and inside the claim with an 8-block margin. Why these numbers (food balance, so the 90 % assertion tests the farm and not the starter kit):
- Population: 8 villagers at seed 1 (M2 spike), maybe 10-12 by day 10 with vanilla breeding (4 new House beds) → 80-120 nutrition a day.
- Kit: 40 bread = 200 nutrition ≈ 2 days. From day 3 the village lives on the field.
- Field: 80 carrot plants, mature at start. Carrot loot ≈ 2.7 per plant, 1 replanted → ≈ 137 carrots on day 1. Regrowth on
  moist farmland in a full field ≈ 1/6 chance per random tick, ≈ 17.6 random ticks per block per day → ≈ 2.4 days per cycle →
  ≈ 57 carrots a day on average. Greedy carrots overshoot (4 carrots = 12 for a need of 10), so count **meals**, not nutrition: an adult
  meal is 4 carrots or 2 bread. Over 10 days: kit 20 meals + day-1 harvest ≈ 34 + 9 days × ≈ 14 ≈ 180 adult meals, against 80-120 needed
  (8-12 villagers). Food carries over in the chests and the assertion is over the whole window, so the cumulative margin (1.5-2.2×)
  is what counts; the thinnest point is days 3-4, before the first regrowth, while most of the day-1 buffer is still there.
  Raw carrots are *prepared* food (3), so no cook is needed. (A wheat field would feed nobody before M13's bread.)
- Diamond axe: 1561 uses. A stone axe (131) would break around day 3-4 and "logs every day" would test tool wear (M15), not M11.
- Grove: 8 oaks ≈ 40-50 logs plus the plains trees within 32 blocks; replanted oak saplings need 2 successful random ticks
  (1/7 each, ≈ 2.5 a day) → most regrow within a day, so there are trees to fell every day.
- Logs: nothing consumes logs before M13's cook (fuel), so "the stockpile's log count rises every day" = "at least one log delivered
  per day". The day-1 comparison is against the baseline (0).

Unseeded-AI robustness: hunger cannot kill (no damage code exists, and `deaths.cause.minecraft:starve` makes that observable; that
roadmap assertion cannot fail by design, so `max(food.max_hungry_days) < 3 from day 3` is the non-vacuous one: nobody goes 3 dinners in a
row without food, which hungriest-first guarantees whenever food is merely short); the ratio
is over the whole window, not per day, so one bad evening (late deposit, a newborn) does not fail it; `food.dinners == 1` makes a
missing dinner loud instead of making the ratio vacuous (0 ≥ 0.9·0 would pass); the hire teleport and day-1 `employed == 2` remove the
"walked into a ravine" variance; the compound is lit so mobs spawn outside it; workers sleep behind doors in Houses (zombies do not
break doors on Normal). What remains is real variance (a zombie kills a worker in the open at dusk): see risk 1.

Run time: ~50 s per day measured for `bare` (TESTING) → 10 days ≈ 8.5 min plus ~1 min boot/setup; two workers, dinner and the extra
entities may add 10-30 % → **budget 10-12 min** (script timeout 240 + 150·10 = 1740 s). Never run it while `verify.sh` or another
simulation runs in the same worktree (`build/run/sim` lock), and give a concurrent run in another checkout its own `SIM_REPORT_DIR`
(BACKLOG simfix item, documented in step 10).

## 6. Risks & unknowns, out of scope

**Risks** (highest first):
1. **(top) `starter` depends on two unbuilt jobs behaving in a real, unseeded village for 10 days.** M10 has no plan; its farmer must
   harvest a 9×9 field within the work day, keep carrots, and deposit before evening; M9's lumberjack must find the grove. A worker
   killed by a zombie at dusk (M2's first `bare` run lost 3 of 8 villagers on night one) breaks food or logs for the rest of the run.
   Mitigations: T-L2-12 proves the layout in verify; teleported hires; a lit compound; Houses with doors; ratio over the window;
   2× food margin. If T-L3-1 fails, the builder reports the per-day numbers and the death causes and does **not** loosen an
   assertion (CLAUDE.md rule 4). If the cause is a mob death of a worker, the agreed next step is for the orchestrator to choose
   between `difficulty peaceful` for `starter` (mobs are M18/M25's subject; `defended` would then turn them back on) and a
   re-hire action; the plan does not decide that in advance because it changes what `starter` means.
2. **Deposit timing vs dinner.** M7 deposits at GO_HOME (from 11000) or when a full stack is carried; food harvested late in the day
   reaches Storage after that day's dinner. The kit covers day 1-2 and the field harvest arrives in full stacks during the day, so this
   only shifts food by a day. Not fixed (moving dinner later would contradict DESIGN §4/§6).
3. **Vanilla breeding** adds eaters (4 new House beds, vanilla farmers carry bread). Babies need 5. Accounted for in the margin; the
   population is reported per day.
4. **Site search on real terrain.** The seed-1 surroundings are unseen. If no 44×32 site exists within 60 blocks with deviation ≤ 3,
   the run ERRORs at setup (never a false pass); the builder then relaxes `max_distance` or the deviation (stated in the deviations).
5. **Rehome eviction fights vanilla.** An evicted resident's vanilla `AcquirePoi` may grab the same bed back before the worker's
   `take` (same tick: no; the take happens immediately after the release). Measured by T-L2-5.
6. **M10 assumptions** (A3) may be wrong in detail; only the planner row (3.6), T-L2-7 and the `field` crop choice depend on them.
7. **Run time** (10-12 min) and CPU contention: L3 is run by hand only, never concurrently with verify in the same worktree.

**Out of scope** (later milestones): wages, strikes and the treasury (M12: replaces `PaidSource`); the cook and cooked-food yields (M13:
compares `food.eaten_nutrition / food.eaten_items` with this milestone's baseline); "fed N days in a row" for tiers (M14 reads
`food.*`/`Diet`); breeding control and "unhoused cannot have children" (M23 reads `Housing.housed` and `FoodMath.daysLeft`); Overview
and Villagers-tab UI for food days left and needs icons beyond what M8 already renders; walking to a table or Storage to eat, eating
particles and sounds; a starving-alert text (M8/M25 alerts); House bed preference for unemployed villagers; a top-up meal optimiser
(M26 balance); `ReportCompare`/`--compare` for M13 (M13's own work).
